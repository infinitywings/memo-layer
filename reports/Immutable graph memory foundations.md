# Classical theory settles validity; edge capture does not

An append-only, typed, immutable knowledge graph whose validity is computed from justifications is, formally, a monotone justification-based truth maintenance system (JTMS) recomputed over an event log, and that core rests on settled ground: with Horn-only justifications the set of valid nodes is a unique least fixed point, independent of processing order and computable in time linear in nodes plus edges, and classical reason maintenance already treats justifications as immutable and retracts only assumptions. Every 2026 system that withdraws dependents (MemTX, PACMI, dependency-guided rollback repair, and the veritas-tms library) follows the same division of labour, in which the model proposes nodes and edges and code decides validity; the three with evaluations beat their baselines (in rollback repair's case including deletion of the retrieved faulty memories and a full reset), but all three are preprints scored on their authors' own benchmarks, and all of them repair only the derivations that were recorded. The open problems therefore sit on either side of the validity function. Upstream is edge capture: PACMI's dependency edges are supplied by annotation, MemTX fails every case in which the agent copies content without declaring its parent, and only rollback repair logs edges from the runtime. In the middle is the meaning of "learned under a decision", a scope relation that over-retracts unless paired with an independent-support check. Downstream is delivery: models that recognise a belief as outdated still act on it (on STALE, Gemini-3.1-pro states that a belief is outdated 92.0% of the time but rejects a query that presupposes it 30.0% of the time). No system found types a decision as a retraction root, composes notes by traversing immutable nodes, or measures how memory mutability affects prompt-cache hit rates, and the cache evidence shows that append-only memory preserves the cache only across session boundaries and concurrent sessions, because a node appended ahead of the conversation invalidates everything behind it exactly as a rewrite does. Anthropic's "The components of agent memory" occupies the opposite pole (model-written notes and wiki pages, untyped links, in-place rewriting by a dreaming pass that checks notes against transcripts) and has vendor-measured gains (Opus 5 mean reward 0.564 to 0.828 on CL-Bench), where the immutable-graph design has none yet. The evidence implies building the validity function conservatively, spending design effort on runtime-logged edges and an independent-support check, ranking withholding of invalid nodes above cache stability, and building the planted-root benchmark that no one has published.

*Status labels used below: [classical] pre-LLM peer-reviewed, standards or canonical practitioner work; [peer-reviewed] LLM-era work at a venue confirmed in the notes; [preprint] arXiv or technical report with no confirmed venue; [workshop] workshop paper or poster; [vendor] company documentation, blogs, issue trackers or vendor-run measurements; [software] self-described library releases; [unverified] claims the notes took from search summaries, abstracts or secondary sources, or venues they could not confirm. Almost every 2026 system discussed is a preprint evaluated on its authors' own benchmark, and none has been independently replicated. Numbers are reported exactly as the source notes give them.*

## Reason maintenance already defines a unique, linear-time validity function

The design's validity rule, that a node stands while its recorded justifications stand, is Doyle's 1979 justification-based truth maintenance system [classical] ([AITopics](https://aitopics.org/doc/classics:CA80645E)). In de Kleer's summary, every datum is a node, every inference step is a justification with an in-list and an out-list, and a node is IN when some justification has all in-list nodes IN and all out-list nodes OUT; the labelling must also give each node **well-founded support, which rules out circular supports that satisfy the justifications only locally** ([de Kleer 1986](https://www.dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf)). In the simplified JTMS that Forbus teaches [classical; lecture slides, not peer-reviewed], justifications are Horn clauses, premises are always IN, and justifications themselves cannot be retracted because they are the problem solver's cache; only assumptions are retracted, by marking the assumption OUT, propagating outness to everything that rests on it, and then searching for alternate support, with an explicit warning that naive re-support through a cycle loses well-founded support ([Forbus](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~forbus/c44/Lectures/TMS%20Intro.pdf)). McAllester's survey of reason maintenance [classical] gives the same interface: a constraint, once added, is never removed ([McAllester, AAAI-90](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1990/AAAI90-164.pdf)). **Immutability is the classical default, not a departure from it.** An append-only justification log, with amendments that defeat assumptions, is how reason maintenance was designed to work. (Most sources give Doyle's pages as 231–272, Wikipedia as 251–272; the primary PDF could not be machine-read, so Doyle's own taxonomy of circular arguments is not reported here ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reason_maintenance)).)

The choice of justification language decides cost and determinism. Boolean constraint propagation, the propagation mechanism of McAllester's clause-based TMS, runs in time linear in the size of a clausal constraint set but is logically incomplete ([McAllester](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1990/AAAI90-164.pdf)). Over a Horn database with n literals and O(n) clauses, **a JTMS finds a solution in linear time, answers single-context queries in constant time with O(n²) space, and updates in O(n)** ([Provan 1988](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/tr/1988/TR-88-11.pdf)) [classical; technical report]. Out-list justifications ("believe X unless Y is believed") change the picture: labelling becomes NP-complete, also incrementally (Elkan 1990 [classical]), beliefs become order-sensitive, and odd loops of non-monotonic justifications do not converge and are expensive to detect ([DBLP](https://dblp1.uni-trier.de/rec/journals/ai/Elkan90.html); [TU Wien RR-1702](https://haendel.kr.tuwien.ac.at/tr-reports/files/rr1702.pdf); [Forbus](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~forbus/c44/Lectures/TMS%20Intro.pdf); [de Kleer](https://www.dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf)). With Horn-only justifications the IN set is the least fixed point of forward propagation from premises and enabled assumptions, so it is unique and independent of the order in which the log is processed. That property is what lets validity be a pure function of the log.

The assumption-based TMS (ATMS) [classical] answers a different question. Each node's label is the set of minimal assumption environments from which it is derivable, kept consistent, sound, complete and minimal, so membership in a context is a subset test, and circular justifications need no special check because any environment that travels round a loop is a superset of one already present ([de Kleer](https://www.dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf)). The cost is size. With n assumptions there are 2^n potential contexts; an ATMS can need exponential time and space for a single query even over acyclic Horn clauses; generating minimal supports is exponential for almost all Boolean expressions; and label explosion drove later compressed and focused variants ([McAllester](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1990/AAAI90-164.pdf); [Provan](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/tr/1988/TR-88-11.pdf); [DeCoste and Collins 1991](https://www.qrg.northwestern.edu/papers/Files/QRG_Dist_Files/QRG_1991/2_124_DeCoste_1991_CATMS_Label_Explosions.pdf)). For the design, "is this node valid?" is a JTMS label and cheap, while "which roots does it rest on?" is an ATMS label and potentially exponential, so the second should be computed on demand for one node, not maintained for the whole graph.

The classical literature also prescribes the amendment mechanism. To make an inference step retractable, de Kleer conjoins its justification with an extra assumption representing its defeasibility and later contradicts that assumption; removing a justification directly forces recursive invalidation of its consequents and possibly the undoing of nogoods, which he advises against ([de Kleer, sect. 4.9](https://www.dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf)). Dependency-directed backtracking (Stallman and Sussman 1977 [classical]) supplies the response to contradiction: justification records identify the premise subset that derived the contradiction, a nogood is installed, and in Doyle's TMS one culprit assumption is chosen ([MaRDI record](https://portal.mardi4nfdi.de/wiki/Publication:1244202); [McAllester](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1990/AAAI90-164.pdf); [de Kleer](https://www.dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf)). In this vocabulary an amendment node is a recorded culprit choice: it defeats one assumption, or one justification's defeasibility assumption, and nothing in the log is rewritten.

### Belief bases, grounded semantics and provenance polynomials fit an amendment log; AGM does not

AGM theory [classical] defines contraction and revision over logically closed belief sets, and its Recovery postulate (contracting a belief and re-adding it restores the original set) is the most debated postulate and fails for belief bases ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-belief-revision/)). An immutable store neither needs nor provides Recovery; Kumiho [preprint] rejects it on exactly those grounds while claiming AGM K*2–K*6 and Hansson's Relevance and Core-Retainment, with K*7 and K*8 left open ([Kumiho](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17244v1)). Hansson's belief-base revision fits cleanly: kernel contraction removes p by applying an incision function to every p-kernel, a minimal subset of the base that implies p ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-belief-revision/)). A p-kernel is the same object as an ATMS environment and a minimal why-provenance witness, and the incision function is the culprit choice of dependency-directed backtracking, so an amendment node is a logged incision. Epistemic entrenchment fits only as the policy that chooses the culprit, and it should be an explicit, logged input. veritas-tms [software] shows the alternative: it ranks entrenchment with a fixed weighted score (0.40 source reliability, 0.25 log corroboration, 0.20 confidence, 0.15 recency with a 30-day half-life), which makes replay depend on more than the log ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/veritas-tms/0.11.1/)).

Katsuno and Mendelzon's distinction between revision (new information about an unchanged world) and update (the world changed) ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-belief-revision/)) is the field an amendment node must carry. **A revision amendment says the root was wrong and should withdraw its dependents; an update amendment says the root was right until some time and should close a validity interval without invalidating conclusions drawn while it held.** Treating every amendment as a revision would withdraw knowledge that was correct for its period. P-Mem [preprint] makes the same split with separate `accept` (supersede) and `reject`/`revoke` operations ([P-Mem](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25054v1)), and Zep's bi-temporal `t_valid`/`t_invalid` edges are the update half of it ([Zep](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13956)) [preprint].

Dung's grounded semantics [classical] gives the amendments-as-attacks reading: the grounded extension is the least fixed point of the characteristic function, always exists, and is computable in O(|V| + |E|) ([arXiv 1405.0406](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.0406); [Nofal, Atkinson and Dunne 2019](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3062427/)), whereas stable and preferred semantics allow several labellings, and odd-length cycles can leave a framework with no stable extension (restated by later papers; not matched to a single primary source) ([arXiv 1907.09426](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.09426.pdf)). If an amendment may target only strictly earlier nodes, the attack relation is acyclic and the semantics coincide on one labelling; the notes flag that this well-founded-framework result was not re-verified from Dung's paper.

Provenance semirings [classical; Green, Karvounarakis and Tannen, PODS 2007] supply the read side. Annotating tuples with polynomials over input identifiers is the most general case, and every other annotation factors through it. **Why-provenance, the set of all contributing inputs, cannot distinguish an output derivable from one input alone from an output that needs two inputs jointly**, which is the paper's own example; positive Boolean expressions (PosBool) keep that distinction and absorb cycles ([Green et al.](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~green/papers/pods07.pdf)). This is the formal reason the design's rule that "nodes with independent support survive" cannot be implemented with lineage. Exact computation is hard under recursion: deciding membership in the why-provenance of recursive Datalog is NP-complete in data complexity, while minimal-why provenance is tractable ([Calautti et al., arXiv 2303.12773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12773v1)) [peer-reviewed, PODS 2024].

The same machinery covers the circularity that matters most for agent memory. Classical well-foundedness, ATMS minimality and PosBool absorption stop a node from supporting itself through recorded edges, but if memory M is delivered to a session and that session's outcome is later counted as evidence for M, the loop is invisible unless the outcome's justification lists M as an antecedent. The "Echo Gap" paper [preprint] formalises the statistical version: when stored episodes are scored by the agent's own assessment, wrong episodes receive inflated scores and are preferentially reused, a second confirming judge does not help because its errors correlate with the first, and an Error-Independence Assumption is necessary for correction; on BIRD text-to-SQL, no memory scores 52.4%, self-graded memory 54.0% and the de-inflated variant 56.9% ([arXiv 2608.00017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00017)). Xiong et al. document the behavioural side, "experience-following" with Pearson r ≈ 1 between input similarity and output similarity for one agent, and show that storing every execution scores below a frozen memory on all four agents (EHRAgent 13.05 against 16.75, AgentDriver 32.32 against 40.11, CIC-IoT 59.90 against 71.50, RegAgent 55.48 against 67.53) ([arXiv 2505.16067v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067v2)). The notes disagree on that paper's status: one treats it as a preprint, another records an ACL 2026 long-paper listing in an Anthology preview that was not opened ([ACL Anthology preview](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.acl-long.27/)), and this project's earlier report cited it as peer-reviewed. No source found formalises "what was in the agent's context when this was learned" as a justification antecedent.

## Models update the fact and miss its consequences, so closure belongs in code

Every LLM-era system found divides the work the same way: the model produces statements, edges, contradiction judgements or operation types, and code computes what follows. REFLEX [peer-reviewed, EMNLP 2023] has a frozen T5-11B build a per-question belief graph of statements and entailment, XOR and multiple-choice rules, then solves weighted MaxSAT for the cheapest set of belief flips; consistency rises from 87.0 to 96.1 on EntailmentBank, 88.2 to 95.9 on OBQA and 85.7 to 96.6 on QuaRTz while answer accuracy stays flat (79.4 to 79.9, 74.0 to 75.0, 80.2 to 80.0) ([arXiv 2305.14250](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14250)). Its failure analysis sets the ceiling every such system inherits: **about 30% of failures come from missing rules, about 30% from incorrect beliefs and about 10% from incorrect rules**, while the solver takes under 1 ms per graph against about 2 s per LLM call (the paper gives graph size as over 350 nodes in the main text and about 600 in the appendix). ArgLLMs [peer-reviewed, AAAI 2025] compute claim strength deterministically from LLM-generated arguments and base scores; accuracy is comparable to baselines, not better (GPT-4o mini 0.81 against 0.78 direct on TruthfulClaim, 0.71 against 0.74 on MedClaim), and the gain is a contestable, editable reasoning trail ([arXiv 2405.02079v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02079v3)). REFLEX is coherentist, in that any belief may flip to restore consistency and one new input can change many labels; a TMS is foundationalist, in that beliefs fall only when their support falls. Only the second reading yields a deterministic, replayable function of the log.

The case for computing closure in code rests on consistent evidence that models handle the changed fact and not its consequences. On MQuAKE-CF [peer-reviewed, EMNLP 2023] with GPT-J, **ROME reaches 88.3% edit-wise success but 7.4% multi-hop accuracy, MEMIT 96.2% and 7.0%, MEND 73.2% and 8.0%**, against 40.5% for the unedited model on the original multi-hop questions, and MEMIT's multi-hop accuracy with chain-of-thought falls from 11.8 to 5.4 as edits grow from 1 to 3,000 ([arXiv 2305.14795](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14795)). Keeping edits outside the weights helps without solving the problem: MeLLo, which checks each sub-answer against a retrieved edit store, falls from 38.9 to 14.2 with GPT-J and from 52.1 to 30.9 with GPT-3.5 as edits accumulate. RippleEdits [peer-reviewed, TACL] finds in-context editing the strongest baseline (82.5 to 90.3 average), yet logical generalisation stays at 33.3 to 71.1 ([arXiv 2307.12976v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.12976v2)). In-context belief revision shows a trade-off rather than a solution: on Belief-R [peer-reviewed, EMNLP 2024], GPT-4 Turbo updates correctly in 16.76% of cases and maintains correctly in 86.72% ([arXiv 2406.19764](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19764)), and AGM-Bench [workshop, ICLR 2026] reports "belief inertia" and "collateral damage" under iterated revision without publishing rates ([ICLR 2026](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/10017503)).

STALE [preprint] isolates the agent-memory case most relevant to the design. When an update to one attribute should silently invalidate a dependent belief (its "Type II" scenarios), **Gemini-3.1-pro falls from 92.0 / 30.0 / 71.0 to 69.0 / 14.0 / 55.0 on its three probes, and Mem0's state resolution falls from 17.0 to 3.5** ([arXiv 2605.06527](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06527v1)). An explicit dependency edge turns a Type II case into a lookup. Singh [preprint] adds a caution about the benchmarks themselves: per-attribute Bayesian belief updating gives little over last-write-wins on LoCoMo because the benchmark rarely contains contradictory or differently reliable evidence, and the same outputs differ by 27.5 points between token-F1 and LLM-judge scoring ([arXiv 2606.22030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22030)). Benchmarks that never retract anything cannot reward a retraction mechanism.

## Immutable data models make "current" a view and deletion an out-of-band act

Eight mature data models express change by adding records and derive current state by a fold, an index or a head pointer. Fowler's event sourcing [classical; practitioner] treats state as a cache derivable from the event log and lists complete rebuild, temporal query and replay-with-correction as consequences, with the caveat that replay must stub external systems ([Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html)). Datomic [vendor] stores immutable five-tuple datoms; a retraction is a later datom with Op false, the original assertion stays, and every database value is a point-in-time immutable snapshot, although the same documentation calls Datomic indelible and chronological yet not append-only, without the performance characteristics of append-only systems, so an immutable logical model does not imply an append-only physical layout ([Datomic](https://docs.datomic.com/whatis/data-model.html)). Git [classical; practitioner] makes every changed object a new content-addressed object ([Pro Git](https://git-scm.com/book/en/v2/Git-Internals-Git-Objects)). W3C PROV [classical; W3C Recommendation 2013] defines `wasDerivedFrom`, `wasRevisionOf` and `wasInvalidatedBy`, treats a changed thing as a new entity, and lets bundles carry provenance of provenance ([PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/)). Nanopublications [classical lineage] can never be edited or deleted; a retraction is a new nanopublication with `npx:retracts`, valid only when signed with the original key, so a third party can only publish a competing claim ([nanopub docs](https://nanopub.readthedocs.io/en/latest/publishing/retraction.html)). RDF 1.2 triple terms let a reifier annotate a proposition without asserting it (Candidate Recommendation Draft, 13 November 2026) ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf12-concepts/)). Together these give the design a vocabulary: Datomic's assert/retract for amendments, nanopublication signing rules for retraction authority among several writers, and PROV edge names for derivation, revision and invalidation.

Incremental view maintenance [classical] is the database counterpart of TMS retraction. The counting algorithm of Gupta, Mumick and Subrahmanian tracks how many derivations each derived tuple has and drops it when the count reaches zero, but fails on recursive rules; delete-and-rederive (DRed) over-deletes everything downstream and re-derives whatever still has support; DBSP represents a deletion as a row of weight −1 in a Z-set ([Hu, Motik and Horrocks, arXiv 1711.03987](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.03987); [DBSP, arXiv 2203.16684](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.16684)) [unverified: both from search summaries]. Support counting answers "which derived summaries must be recomputed when a source is retracted"; summaries of summaries are the recursive case where plain counting fails and DRed or weighted deltas are needed. JTMS retraction is DRed under another name.

Deletion is out of band in every model, and that separates retraction from erasure. Datomic's excision removes datoms permanently for privacy and retention, leaves a permanent record that it happened, burdens background indexing, and the documentation advises against excising more than a few thousand datoms at a time; it also states that "excision should never be used to correct erroneous data" ([Datomic, Excision](https://docs.datomic.com/operation/excision.html)). Crypto-shredding deletes a per-subject key, but the legal status is disputed: Verraes records a lawyer's view that encrypted personal data remains personal data under GDPR, while a vendor blog claims a national regulator endorses it; the notes could verify neither position ([Verraes](https://verraes.net/2019/05/eventsourcing-patterns-throw-away-the-key/); [Conduktor](https://conduktor.io/blog/gdpr-kafka-right-to-erasure)). Anthropic's Managed Agents memory store [vendor] can redact historical versions while preserving who did what and when, but not the current head ([Anthropic memory docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/memory)). Content addressing and redaction pull against each other: if node identity is a hash of content, removing content changes every identifier that includes it, so a design that needs both should hash a reference to a separately deletable payload. The consequence of having no erasure path is measured: on Bemula [preprint], **no memory system passes more than 1.5% of explicit forget requests end to end**, and the named causes include append-only stores with no removal path and redundant copies of the same fact as raw turns, extracted facts and compressed notes ([arXiv 2610.18052](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18052v1)).

LLM-agent systems realise immutability at three levels that should not be conflated. Level (a) keeps a mutable head over immutable history: Anthropic's memory versions (immutable, attributed to the writing session, kept 30 days, with no restore endpoint) ([Anthropic memory docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/memory)) [vendor], Letta's git-backed context repositories ([Letta](https://www.letta.com/blog/context-repositories)) [vendor] and GCC's revised `main.md` ([GCC](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00031v2)) [preprint]. Level (b) keeps an immutable log with LLM-derived views: Zep/Graphiti, DISCERN's append-only per-actor log with rebuildable views ([DISCERN](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.15729)) [preprint], and Anthropic's Dreams, which writes a new store and never modifies its inputs ([Dreams docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/dreams)) [vendor]. Level (c) keeps an immutable log with deterministic views: OpenHands' event replay, ACE's non-LLM delta merge and P-Mem's readout. **The design is level (c), the least populated.** OpenHands [peer-reviewed, MLSys 2026] gives the only measured costs: per-event persist 0.20 ms median, full replay 4.1 ms median and 18.9 ms at the maximum of 358 events, 380 KB median storage per conversation, with condensation recorded as an event so the full log survives compression ([arXiv 2511.03690v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03690v2)).

### Rewriting in place loses measured accuracy, and append-only storage alone recovers only part of it

The evidence against LLM rewrite-in-place is now direct. ACE [peer-reviewed, ICLR 2026] documents "context collapse": on AppWorld with Dynamic Cheatsheet, **an 18,282-token context at 66.7% accuracy became 122 tokens at 57.1% one step later, below the 63.7% no-adaptation baseline**, and ACE's incremental deltas beat full rewriting by 7.6 points online ([arXiv 2510.04618v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618v3)). "Useful Memories Become Faulty When Continuously Updated by LLMs" [preprint] ran the head-to-head ablation with GPT-5.4 on ARC-AGI Stream at step 400: in-place consolidation 43.2%, append-only with the prior store visible 50.0%, add and delete without in-place edit 64.0%, append-only with the prior store hidden 70.0%, and raw trajectories with no consolidation 76.6%; in a repeated-run check, consolidated memory fell from 55.1 to 38.5 pass@1 between epochs 1 and 10 against 58.5 with no memory ([arXiv 2605.12978v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12978v2)). The paper is internally inconsistent on one headline (its abstract says GPT-5.4 fails on 54% of previously solved problems, its body gives about 46%). The ordering separates two harms: forbidding in-place edits recovers some accuracy, while stopping the consolidator from conditioning on its own earlier abstractions recovers much more. HaluMem [preprint] locates the commercial failure in omission rather than fabrication: update omission rates of 74.02% (Mem0), 52.31% (Zep) and 37.48% (MemOS) against update hallucination of at most 1.15% ([arXiv 2511.03506v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03506v3)); its authors include MemOS's vendor and MemOS ranks first, so the ranking deserves caution. An immutable source layer makes omission recoverable by later re-extraction; a rewritten store has discarded the source.

Append-only storage alone is not the answer, and one preprint argues the opposite case. Infini Memory [preprint] rewrites an append buffer into topic documents because append-style storage leaves both versions retrievable, and reports MemoryAgentBench overall 64.7 against 45.5 for HippoRAG-v2 ([arXiv 2606.10677](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10677v1)). P-Mem measures the same risk: raw top-10 retrieval over revision chains of length 1, 2, 4 and 8 fell to 100, 72.9, 42.9 and 25.0%, while its committed graph stayed at 100% ([P-Mem](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25054v1)). What works in these results is append-only storage plus an explicit, deterministic projection that decides which version is current before anything reaches the model.

### Composing notes from node text is the only point where traceability holds by construction

Free-form generation with citations is far from fully supported. On ALCE [peer-reviewed, EMNLP 2023], even the best models lack complete citation support about half the time on ELI5 (ChatGPT recall 51.1, precision 50.0), and feeding summaries instead of passages raises correctness while lowering citation quality ([arXiv 2305.14627v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14627v2)). "Attribute First, then Generate" [peer-reviewed, ACL 2024] selects source spans before writing: on long-form QA it reaches **0.0% uncited sentences with 48.2 cited tokens per sentence against 26.9% and 2,153.3 for the ALCE baseline**, AutoAIS 89.3 against 49.8, and cuts human verification time per sentence from 59 s to 35 s ([arXiv 2403.17104](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2403.17104)). The extractive-abstractive spectrum study [peer-reviewed, TACL 2026; figures unverified, from a search summary] finds perceived utility rises by over 200% from extractive to abstractive output while citation coverage falls by over 50% ([arXiv 2411.17375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17375)). Anthropic's Citations API [vendor] guarantees valid pointers into supplied documents, not that a sentence is entailed by them ([Citations docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations)). For the design, a note composed by traversal and rendered as the concatenated text of its nodes is traceable by construction; any LLM rewriting step reopens the support gap and needs its own entailment check, and derived text should cite leaf evidence, not intermediate summaries.

## Graph and atomic-note memories type their links for retrieval, not for retraction

Almost every surveyed graph or note memory stores LLM-written units whose types and links are free-form model output, and most of them rewrite or delete in place. A-MEM [peer-reviewed, NeurIPS 2025] links notes without types, and its "memory evolution" overwrites neighbours' context, keywords and tags with no history ([arXiv 2502.12110](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12110v11)). Mem0 [preprint; vendor] chooses ADD, UPDATE, DELETE or NOOP per fact with no versioning, while its graph variant marks relations invalid instead of removing them ([arXiv 2504.19413](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19413v1)). Zep [preprint; vendor] keeps invalidated edges with four timestamps but rewrites entity and community summaries, which drift and need periodic full refreshes ([arXiv 2501.13956](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13956v1)); current Graphiti documentation adds Pydantic entity and edge types with an `edge_type_map`, but classifying text into those types is still done by the extraction model and code checks only attribute shape ([Graphiti docs](https://help.getzep.com/graphiti/core-concepts/custom-entity-and-edge-types)). HippoRAG 2 [peer-reviewed, ICML 2025] only appends, MemTree [peer-reviewed, ICLR 2025] rewrites every ancestor on insertion at about 3.27 LLM calls per insert, and AriGraph [peer-reviewed, IJCAI 2025] deletes outdated triplets ([HippoRAG 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14802v2); [MemTree](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14052); [AriGraph](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04363)). Types are assigned by code mainly where the source is formal: RepoGraph and LocAgent build node and edge types with a parser ([LocAgent](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.09089)) [peer-reviewed, ACL 2025]. **Edge vocabularies split into an associative family (semantic, temporal, entity, contains) and a dependency family (depends-on, derived-from, supports, used-by, supersedes); only the second supports pruning, and none of the widely used or peer-reviewed systems has it.** No surveyed system composes a note at read time from compact immutable nodes plus amendments; Kumiho's revisions each replace the whole previous revision, and Zep rewrites its summaries.

Atomic units help retrieval and hurt answers when they replace the source. Dense X Retrieval [peer-reviewed, EMNLP 2024] raises Recall@5 from 34.3 to 46.3 (SimCSE) and 43.0 to 52.7 (Contriever) by indexing propositions instead of passages, yet even GPT-4 leaves 4.9% of propositions not stand-alone ([arXiv 2312.06648](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.06648)). LongMemEval [unverified venue] finds that storing extracted facts as the value loses information, while using them to expand the index key and keeping the original value improves recall@k by 9.4% and accuracy by 5.4% on average ([arXiv 2410.10813](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10813)). Han et al. [peer-reviewed, KDD 2026] show a triplets-only knowledge graph scoring 34.28 F1 on NQ against 64.78 for plain RAG, with only about 65.8% of HotpotQA answer entities present in the LLM-built graph ([arXiv 2502.11371](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371)). Anthropic's own contract experiment points the same way: one page per contract with terms quoted verbatim preserved **57 of 57 graded facts, where a shared, jointly edited table kept 41 of 57 (72%)** ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)) [vendor]. The supported recipe is compact nodes as index keys and link endpoints, each pointing to the source episode it came from.

LLM-built graphs carry measurable defects that typing addresses only in part. Without normalisation, GraphRAG produced 966 edge types for 981 relations over 514 entities, so free-form labels cannot drive traversal rules ([KGGen, arXiv 2502.09956](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09956)) [preprint; NeurIPS 2025 inferred, unverified]. A closed edge vocabulary checked by code is the direct remedy for that defect. It does not touch extraction error: HippoRAG 2 traces 26% of its failures to the LLM triple filter, RAPTOR found minor hallucinations in about 4% of sampled summary nodes, and HaluMem puts extraction accuracy below 62% for every system ([HippoRAG 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14802v2); [RAPTOR](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.18059); [HaluMem](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03506)). Code-checked types guarantee well-formedness, not truth.

Traversal is not automatically better than flat search, and the sources disagree. MemTree and RAPTOR both found flat retrieval over all nodes beat structure-following traversal, and Mem0's graph variant lowered multi-hop judge score from 51.15 to 47.19 while doubling stored tokens ([MemTree](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14052); [RAPTOR](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.18059); [Mem0](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19413v1)). Against that, A-MEM's links account for most of its gain (multi-hop F1 9.65 without links, 21.35 with link generation, 27.02 for the full system), and MAGMA [preprint] reports typed, intent-weighted traversal at 0.700 on LoCoMo against 0.580 for A-MEM in a single unreplicated paper ([A-MEM](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12110v11); [MAGMA](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03236)). Token savings from compact units replicate across papers (Zep about 1.6k context tokens against 115k full context; MAGMA 0.7 to 4.2k against about 101k), but accuracy rankings do not, across papers that are all preprints or vendor posts: Zep's LoCoMo score ranges from 59.22 to 79.09 depending on who ran it, Zep's own rebuttal reports 75.14 ± 0.17, and in Mem0's own paper a full-context baseline (72.90) beats Mem0's best variant (68.44) ([Zep](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13956v1); [MAGMA](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03236); [MemOS](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.03724); [MIRIX](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07957); [Zep blog](https://www.getzep.com/blog/lies-damn-lies-statistics-is-mem0-really-sota-in-agent-memory/)). A design should not be positioned on LoCoMo rank, and composition by traversal should be tested against flat retrieval over the same nodes. The two large surveys classify graph memory by topology and lifecycle, not by mutability, typing authority or dependency structure, and list graph quality without intrinsic metrics, non-transferable schemas, provenance tracking and unsupervised updates among open problems ([arXiv 2512.13564](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13564v2); [arXiv 2602.05665](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05665v1)) [both preprints].

## Wrong roots arrive early, persist, and resist capture

The decisive error in a failed run is usually early and epistemic. In 1,184 failed CLI coding-agent runs, "Failure as a Process" [preprint] places the decisive error at **median step 7 against a median failed-run length of 27**, attributes 30.7% of decisive errors to false premises, finds observable signals about 10 steps after the error, and finds that 82% of failed runs keep executing without progress; a real-time prefix monitor flags only 3.7 to 8.7% of failures before lock-in ([arXiv 2607.09510](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09510)). A trajectory study of 95 failed multi-agent runs [preprint; LLM-annotated, no human agreement reported] finds the deviated state retained and reused in 74 runs, a contradicting observation present before the symptom in 68 runs and ignored or never mentioned in 60 of those, and no response that removed the deviated state (4 removed part of it) ([arXiv 2611.13254](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13254)). Automated attribution is weak: the best method on Who&When [unverified venue] names the decisive step 14.2% of the time ([arXiv 2505.00212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00212)). These numbers describe a wrong root on which many later steps are built before anyone notices, and they also mark the design's boundary: tracing helps only after something declares the root wrong.

Once written, a wrong memory stays until an external trigger removes it. Malicious memory persists in 84.2% of MemSecBench cases [preprint], and selective repair succeeds in 56.1% of poisoned cases ([arXiv 2607.27080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27080)). In "When Stale Constraints Go Unchecked" [preprint; carried over from earlier notes], agents made stale-consistent decisions 74.7 to 77.3% of the time while inspecting a reachable provenance path in about 17% of episodes, and forcing one verification slot onto the critical path added 61 to 81 points ([arXiv 2608.25553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25553)). In 20,574 real coding-agent sessions [preprint; abstract only], 91.49% of visible misalignment resolutions needed explicit user correction ([arXiv 2605.29442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29442)). Anthropic's Mt. Moon case [vendor] is the one documented model-side correction: a note that misplaced a landmark sent Claude looping until a dreaming pass checked it against the transcript and rewrote it, after which the corrected run reached Cerulean City at action 1,935 ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)). Dreaming fixes notes that contradict the record; it has no stated mechanism for notes that were faithful to the transcript but learned under a route later found wrong.

Checkpoints restore state; they do not withdraw beliefs. Claude Code [vendor] snapshots files and conversation before each prompt, keeps the 100 most recent checkpoints, does not track files changed by shell commands, and says nothing about memory ([Claude Code checkpointing](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/checkpointing)). In the 95-run study, restoring the agent's state before the fault origin would have sufficed in 35 runs, while in 56 the fault sat in a plan, brief or stop rule that a restore would replay ([arXiv 2611.13254](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13254)). Route-level withdrawal keeps time moving and removes by dependency; rollback discards everything after a point, true or not.

Capturing decisions has been the weak link for fifty years. Nygard's architecture decision records [classical; practitioner] keep context, decision, status and consequences, and mark reversed decisions superseded rather than deleting them, the same stance as the design; but "Consequences" is free text, not a machine-followable link to what was later learned under the decision ([Nygard](https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions)). IBIS, QOC and DRL formalised rationale and ran into the capture problem, the cost of recording rationale without disrupting work ([Wikipedia, Design rationale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_rationale)) [unverified, tertiary]; Shipman and Marshall argue that forcing users into a formalism imposes effort they resist ([author copy](https://people.engr.tamu.edu/shipman/formality-paper/harmful.html)) [classical; unverified summary]. A 2026 study of 5,800 ADRs in 921 repositories [peer-reviewed, ICSA 2026; abstract only] finds about 63% created already "accepted", bypassing the deliberation they exist to record ([ICSA 2026](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icsa-2026/icsa-2026-papers/34/Architecture-Decision-Records-Adoption-Impact-and-Developer-Engagement-in-Open-Sou)). Post-hoc extraction does not close the gap: LLM-generated design rationale scores precision 0.267 to 0.278 against recall 0.627 to 0.715 ([arXiv 2504.20781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20781)) [unverified], and AssumptionMiner [preprint] reaches F1 0.816 at category level but 0.66 at decision level ([arXiv 2607.22898](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22898)). Anthropic's article shows the free-text version emerging spontaneously, with stronger models storing a current goal, a plan and rules together with how they knew each rule was true, but its wiki links are navigational and record no dependency between a plan and notes learned under it ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)). The nearest structures to "decision node plus learned-under edges" are GCC's branches, which record purpose with no retraction semantics, and MemTrace's sealed coding traces with depend, verify, correct and supersede edges but no decision type and no cascade (DeepSWE pass@1 56.6% against 35.4% native) ([GCC](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00031v2); [MemTrace](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04838v1)) [both preprints]. Across these sources, capture channels rank by reliability: runtime-logged events first, verbatim user instructions next, explicit agent tool calls (made well when made, often skipped), post-hoc model extraction, and model self-diagnosis last (0 of 121 Reflexion-style reflections named the correct target in one workshop study) ([arXiv 2605.29463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29463)) [workshop].

## Six 2026 systems each build one piece, and none starts the cascade from a decision

Two families are often conflated. **Key-versioned supersession** (P-Mem, BELIEFMEM, Kumiho, TEPA, Graphiti) resolves conflicts between values of the same key and walks a supersedes chain; **justification-based retraction** (MemTX, PACMI, dependency-guided rollback repair, veritas-tms, and the design) withdraws conclusions whose support is gone. Only the second handles "a root decision was wrong; withdraw what was built on it". The narrative behind the table sharpens three points. First, **edge provenance differs more than any other property**. MemTX's derivation DAG is declared by the agent, and its own limitation section reports that when the agent transcribes content without declaring a parent ("source-scope laundering"), every backbone fails every case; PACMI's edges come from annotation, so its benchmark cannot test capture or over-invalidation; only rollback repair obtains edges deterministically from runtime logs, and it deliberately excludes execution-order edges because a later step is not evidence of contamination ([MemTX](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23929v2); [PACMI](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.05732); [rollback repair](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10502v1)). Second, **only rollback repair and veritas-tms check independent support**, and rollback repair's ablation shows what the check buys: without it recovery is 0.880 but benign preservation falls from 1.000 to 0.986 with more replay. Third, **no system starts from a decision node or composes notes by traversal**; MemTX's root is a corrupted record, rollback repair's a diagnosed fault, PACMI's a directly contradicted memory.

The sources disagree on how much the cascade itself matters. MemTX reports about 11 points of task success lost without cascading rollback, and rollback repair's recovery falls to 0.713 without its rollback planner; PACMI's own cascade ablation moves errors from 3 to 11 at p = 0.0574, not significant, in a two-hop, six-memory setup where retrieval is nearly saturated ([MemTX](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23929v2); [rollback repair](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10502v1); [PACMI](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.05732)). The strongest evidence that recorded structure, not model reasoning, makes cascade correctness achievable comes from RECON [preprint; single author]: on its cascade task an oracle given the dependency graph scores 81.8% against 18.2% for the best LLM, and overall memory systems score 9.7 to 15.1% against 54.6% for the oracle and 63.0% for humans ([arXiv 2607.16716](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16716v1)). Graph tracing and model judgment are also complementary rather than ranked: rollback repair beats LLM-judge repair on recovery (0.853 against 0.773) with 43.3% fewer replayed steps, but the LLM judge identifies affected claims far better (claim-invalidation F1 0.946 against 0.566) and leaves less recurrence (0.121 against 0.266) ([rollback repair](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10502v1)). MemTX adds two results a designer should note: its cascade-completeness invariant was checked by property-based tests over 10,000 traces and bounded enumeration of 5,530,160 states, which found one missed cascade on the abort path; and on its strongest backbone (GPT-5.5) it ties the best baseline (0.756 against 0.778) ([MemTX](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23929v2)).

The key-versioned systems contribute different lessons. P-Mem shows that keeping defeated versions with a reopen path matters: recovery of a displaced value after a retraction is 100% with retained history and 0% both without retention and for Graphiti, which keeps the old edge but has no reopen operation; its stale-answer rate on FactConsolidation is 4.7/5.8/4.2% at 6k/64k/262k against Mem0's 43.8% rising to 59.2%; and its authors report about ±10-point intervals per cell, contest and revoke exercised mainly in controlled probes, and write-time extraction that pays for itself only after roughly 70 queries per history ([P-Mem](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25054v1)). (The notes give 52.6 both as P-Mem's LongMemEval-S overall score and as its materialised accuracy with a 4B writer, the latter with a 33.3% stale rate, and do not establish whether these are one measurement.) Kumiho stops at impact analysis, BELIEFMEM applies AGM-style operators to a never-deleting fact base with no propagation to derived beliefs described, and TEPA [preprint] revokes a keyed precedent when contradicting evidence arrives, scoring 0.950 under full regime reversal against 0.210 for both append-only and last-write-wins ([TEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07429)).

## Anthropic's four layers trade structure for measured gains

Lance Martin's "The components of agent memory" [vendor, 2026-11-06] describes four layers: transcripts as a searchable event log, notes that Claude writes into folders under simple guidance, representations (mainly wikis whose pages Claude writes and maintains, with links serving as a map), and injected context loaded into every session and kept small ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)). An offline "dreaming" pass checks notes against transcripts, rewrites wrong ones, writes, corrects and merges wiki pages, repoints broken links and promotes notes into injected context. The article frames a memory as a prediction that the note will matter later and be relied on without re-checking. Its measurements are vendor-run with no reported seeds or variance, and a secondary write-up says several are single runs ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/gabrielanhaia/anthropics-agent-memory-study-rules-on-what-to-save-cost-claude-much-of-the-gain-3089)) [unverified]: on CL-Bench, mean reward rises from 0.655 to 0.838 (Opus 5.5), 0.564 to 0.828 (Opus 5), 0.383 to 0.700 (Opus 4.7) and only 0.330 to 0.364 (Sonnet 4.6); a fixed "record only failures" rule cuts the gain by 0.234 for Opus 5; on the Stainless corpus wiki-only accuracy rises from 13% to 48% of graded criteria as dreaming scales from 2 to 128 sessions, and wiki plus search scores 61% against 53% for search alone while saving about 20,000 tokens per question; cross-document merger questions score 87% with the wiki against 73% search-only, with no clear gain on a single-document task. The independent CL-Bench paper [preprint] is cooler: dedicated memory systems do not beat naive in-context learning, whose best configuration (on Sonnet 4.6) reaches a normalised gain of 25.4% against 8.6% for ACE at $62.8 per rollout ([arXiv 2606.05661](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05661)).

Anthropic already moves toward several of the design's commitments. Transcripts as the foundation correspond to the design's source layer; Managed Agents memory versions are immutable and attributed to the writing session; Dreams never modifies its input store; the contract experiment shows compact, unshared, verbatim units beating a jointly edited table; the dreaming cookbook [vendor; carried over from earlier notes] states that a single session's passing remark never overrides a recorded decision ([cookbook](https://platform.claude.com/cookbook/managed-agents-cma-dreaming-with-a-dynamic-workflow)); and Claude Code freezes memory at session start, which is the frozen-core rendering the cache evidence favours ([Claude Code caching docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/prompt-caching.md)). The divergences are structural. Links are untyped and the article reports only link counts (255 links over 68 pages; 225 to 278 after a gap-filling pass). Dreaming rewrites in place, and Anthropic documents the cost of shared editing (41 of 57 facts surviving) and over-building (a 203 KB wiki from a 115 KB corpus, 1.77 times the source, which abstracted nothing); its knowledge-wiki quickstart reports about 5% of lines filed to the busiest shared page being overwritten ([quickstart](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-quickstarts/tree/main/managed-agents/knowledge-wiki)) [vendor]. Promotion into injected context follows access frequency and size, not validity, although the article itself names a wrong note propagating everywhere as a risk of that layer. Claude Code's four note types are a frontmatter string the model writes, and the only code-side check is on index size (200 lines or 25 KB) ([Claude Code memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)).

Two warnings run from Anthropic toward the design. The "record only failures" result shows that constraining what a strong model writes costs it gain, so a design that makes the model fill typed schemas and declare edges at write time risks the same loss, and should constrain trust and delivery instead, capturing structure through the harness. And the wiki-only accuracy curve shows that LLM synthesis adds value that purely extractive composition may lack, the same utility-against-traceability trade the extractive-abstractive spectrum study measures. In return, the design offers an answer to an open problem the article names, that checking all memories continuously may be impractical: when a root falls, dependency edges scope the check to that root's dependents.

## Prompt caches reward stable bytes only ahead of the conversation and inside the cache lifetime

Every hosted provider and open-source server caches on an exact token prefix from token 0, so one changed token invalidates everything after it. Anthropic [vendor] allows up to 4 breakpoints, builds the prefix in the order tools, system, messages, looks back at most 20 blocks per breakpoint, requires a minimum of 512 to 4,096 tokens depending on model, and prices 5-minute writes at 1.25x and 1-hour writes at 2x base input, with reads at 0.1x by default, 0.05x on Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, and 0.025x on Fable 5.2, Mythos 5.2, Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 ([Anthropic prompt caching](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)). OpenAI [vendor] caches automatically from 1,024 tokens on GPT-5.6 and later, with 30-minute or 12-hour lifetimes whose writes cost 1.25x or 2.5x and reads 0.1x (0.05x for GPT-6.1 Sol and Terra) ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching)). DeepSeek [vendor] has no write premium and, on the pricing page read on 2026-11-13, a 50x hit-to-miss price ratio on `deepseek-flash` and 30x on `deepseek-v4-pro` ([DeepSeek](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/guides/kv_cache); [pricing](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/quick_start/pricing)); Gemini 4 caches the full previous request only when a new request adds at least 2,050 tokens ([Gemini](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/caching)) [vendor]. Reuse of an unchanged segment that follows a changed one exists only in research systems that need control of the inference server, such as CacheBlend [peer-reviewed, EuroSys 2025], with time to first token 2.2 to 3.3x lower than prefix caching, and AgentKVShift [preprint], which refreshes 10 to 30% of the cache for retrieved memory units ([CacheBlend](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16444); [AgentKVShift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21604)).

Production traces show where misses come from. In a GitHub Copilot trace of 3.2M users, 13M sessions and 761M LLM calls [preprint; technical report, venue unconfirmed], the median prompt is 68K tokens of which 63K are cached and the median hit rate is about 98%, but the distribution is bimodal; idle gaps over 10 minutes drop the hit rate to 0 to 5%, a model switch leaves an average of 8%, and **compaction, present in 7.8% of sessions, removes a median 72.8% of prompt tokens and drops the next call's hit rate by a median 66.1%, with 21% of events erasing 99% or more** ([Copilot traces](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/ghcp_traces-6.pdf)); the study mixes percentage points and relative terms in its drop figures. TraceLab [preprint] measures a 95.7% token-weighted hit rate over about 4,265 Claude Code and Codex sessions, 84.4% on user-initiated steps against 97.5% on tool-result steps, with cache reads forming 59.5% of session spend ([TraceLab](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30560)). Lumer et al. [preprint] find caching cuts cost by 77.8 to 81.4% on GPT-5.2 and Claude Sonnet 4.5 but only 27.8 to 41.4% on Gemini 2.5 Pro, and that a prompt below the provider minimum regresses time to first token by 10 to 18% ([arXiv 2601.06007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06007)). Engineering reports converge on the rules: Manus [vendor] calls KV-cache hit rate the most important production metric and keeps context append-only with deterministic serialisation ([Manus](https://manus.im/blog/Context-Engineering-for-AI-Agents-Lessons-from-Building-Manus)); the Claude Code team [vendor] alerts on hit rate, orders layers from static system prompt and tools through memory to conversation, and delivers state changes as reminders in the next message rather than edits to the system prompt ([Claude Code blog](https://claude.com/blog/lessons-from-building-claude-code-prompt-caching-is-everything)).

Memory-specific evidence is thin and comes from vendors and bug reports. Zep [vendor] replayed one scripted conversation with a per-turn memory block placed either in the system prompt or as a trailing message, and found the trailing placement 1.3x cheaper at 18 turns, 1.6x at 36 and 1.9x at 54 ([Zep](https://www.getzep.com/blog/where-you-put-memory-in-the-prompt-can-cut-your-token-bill-up-to-2x/)). A hermes-agent issue [vendor; issue tracker] reports about 1% prefix hit rate on 64K-token prompts when a Mem0 plugin injected retrieval into the system prompt each turn, fixed by appending recall to the user message ([hermes-agent #25971](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/issues/25971)). No peer-reviewed or preprint study measures cache invalidation caused by memory updates or compares append-only with rewritten memory.

The design's cache claim therefore needs a refinement. **Appending a node to a memory block that sits ahead of the conversation invalidates everything behind it exactly as a rewrite would.** Immutable nodes in stable oldest-first order pay off in three places only: across sessions, when session N+1's memory prefix extends session N's within the cache lifetime; across concurrent sessions and subagents that share the prefix; and within a session, only if new nodes and amendments are delivered at the tail as appended messages. Oldest-first is the only order in which adding a node leaves earlier bytes in place; relevance-ordered retrieval re-sorts every turn and belongs in the volatile tail. The Letta forum [vendor; forum] supplies the counter-case: a rewrite performed while the session is idle past the cache lifetime costs nothing in cache terms ([Letta forum](https://forum.letta.com/t/breaking-prompt-caching/149)).

Simple arithmetic from the published prices (derived in the notes, not measured) sets the stakes. One invalidation adds D × (w − r) × p to a request, where D is the number of tokens after the first changed byte; at $5/MTok with 5-minute writes and 0.1x reads, re-writing 100K tokens costs $0.575 instead of $0.05. A consolidating rewrite inside a live cache window that shrinks memory by S tokens breaks even only after 11.5 × D/S further cached turns at 0.1x reads (49 × D/S at 0.025x), about 230 turns to recover from removing 5K tokens ahead of a 100K-token conversation. Mid-session consolidation is almost never worth it on cost; re-basing at a cold boundary is free. This creates **a real conflict with withdrawal**. The cache-preserving way to retract a node already in the cached core is to append a "withdrawn" marker and leave the old bytes until the next re-base, the "mask, don't remove" trade that Manus and Claude Code accept for tools. But the evidence on delivery says marking is not withholding: on STALE, Gemini-3.1-pro recognises an outdated belief 92.0% of the time and rejects a query that presupposes it 30.0% of the time, agents inspect a reachable stale provenance link in about 17% of episodes, and passive "unverified" labels are ignored ([arXiv 2606.29279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29279)) [preprint; carried over]. Because a 100K-token invalidation costs cents, withdrawing a root whose dependents sit in the cached core is precisely the case where the design should break its own cache. Cache stability should govern growth; validity should govern delivery.

## What the evidence implies for the design

The table states the recommended choice for each element, the strongest evidence behind it, and the risk that remains; the paragraphs after it explain the choices that are not obvious.

| Design element | Evidence-backed choice | Strongest evidence (status) | Remaining risk |
|---|---|---|---|

| Amendments | Defeat an assumption or a per-justification defeasibility assumption; stratified (target only earlier nodes); revision vs update field; logged culprit reason; reopen path | de Kleer [classical]; Katsuno–Mendelzon via SEP [classical]; P-Mem reopen 100% vs 0% [preprint] | Authority rules for several writers |
| Dependency edges | Runtime-logged first; code refuses derived nodes without antecedent edges; closed vocabulary; execution order not an edge | Rollback repair [preprint]; MemTX laundering failure [preprint]; KGGen [preprint] | Post-hoc decision extraction F1 0.66 |
| "Learned under" a decision | Scope edge marks candidates; a candidate falls only if no justification avoids the root; observations stay premises | Rollback repair support check [preprint]; RECON [preprint] | Over-retraction if treated as derivation |
| Independent support | Re-derive from premises; minimal supporting root sets on demand with caps; exclude evidence produced under delivery of the same node | Green et al. [classical]; Echo Gap [preprint] | Exact witnesses are hard under recursion |
| Views and notes | Deterministic current-value view; notes composed extractively from node text, citing leaves; derived summaries regenerated from sources | P-Mem chains [preprint]; Attribute First, ALCE [peer-reviewed]; Useful Memories [preprint] | Utility cost of extractive text; traversal untested against flat retrieval |
| Delivery | Withhold invalid nodes; measure behaviour, not store contents | STALE, stale constraints [preprints] | Root detection stays weak (14.2% step attribution) |

| Erasure | Separate excision path; hash a reference to a deletable payload; rebuild views after erasure; record that erasure happened | Datomic [vendor]; Bemula [preprint] | Contested legal status of crypto-shredding |
| Capture burden | Leave writes free; capture decisions through the harness at decision time | Anthropic −0.234 [vendor]; ADRs 63% born accepted [peer-reviewed, abstract only] | Agents skip optional tools |
| Evaluation | Planted-root, multi-session benchmark with dependent, independent and unrelated items | Assembled from rollback repair, MemTX, STALE, Bemula, MemoryArena | None exists |

**Build the validity function to classical specification.** Observations are premises and are never retracted, only excised; decisions and claims adopted without derivation are assumptions; derived claims and procedures are consequents of Horn justifications, each an AND-set of antecedent node identifiers, with several justifications meaning OR. An amendment defeats an assumption, or the defeasibility assumption attached to one justification, so a single inference step can be withdrawn without touching its antecedents. Amendments may target only earlier nodes, and an amendment may itself be defeated only by a later amendment, which keeps the attack relation acyclic and the labelling unique. Re-support after a retraction must be recomputed upward from premises, never by local repair, or a cycle can prop itself up. The notes estimate (by derivation from the linear bounds, not by measurement) that a full relabel of 10^6 nodes with a few antecedents per justification visits on the order of 10^6 to 10^7 edges, sub-second in compiled code and seconds in interpreted code, so full recomputation on every amendment is viable and keeps the function replayable; the only empirical anchors are veritas-tms's self-reported 15,800 assert-with-contraction operations per second at 10^4 beliefs and OpenHands' 4.1 ms median event replay ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/veritas-tms/0.11.1/); [OpenHands](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03690v2)). No published measurement of TMS relabelling at 10^5 to 10^6 nodes exists. Contradiction detection is model-judged in every system surveyed; its output should enter the graph as an ordinary node with its own justification, outside the validity function.

**Treat "learned under a decision" as scope, and test it against independent support.** The phrase describes a temporal or scope relation, not a derivation, and the sources that work with derivation graphs refuse to equate the two: rollback repair excludes execution-order edges from propagation, and MemTrace does not treat a later timestamp as supersession ([rollback repair](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10502v1); [MemTrace](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04838v1)). A pure in-force edge would therefore mark independently true knowledge as dependent. The evidence supports three edge roles: derivation edges, which are justification antecedents and drive the cascade; scope edges, which mark everything learned while a decision was in force as a candidate for review, where a candidate falls only if none of its justifications avoids the root; and delivered-context edges, which record what memory was in the prompt when a node was produced and serve only to exclude self-derived evidence. Delivered-context edges must not drive the cascade, because listing the whole prompt as antecedents makes everything depend on everything. The independence test itself needs minimal supporting root sets, not lineage, computed on demand for one node with caps on count and size, falling back to lineage (cheap, but blind to independence) when a cap is hit. The self-provenance rule follows from well-foundedness and the Error-Independence Assumption: evidence produced in a session where node M was delivered does not count as support for M. This project's own earlier experiment (internal, unpublished) found 45% of support links for promoted lessons coming from sessions that had already received the lesson, which is the loop this rule closes.

**Spend the engineering on edge capture, and measure it separately.** Both MemTX and PACMI name the same weak point, that a cascade is only as complete as the recorded edges, and capture channels differ sharply in reliability. Runtime-logged edges are deterministic for whatever passes through the harness, which argues for deriving edges from tool calls, file reads, test runs and memory reads rather than asking the model to declare them; code that refuses a derived node lacking its required antecedent edges attacks the laundering case directly. Model-declared edges are a fallback, and post-hoc extraction (decision-level F1 0.66 in the best case found) is a last resort. Because the labelling is exact only relative to recorded justifications, missing edges cause under-retraction and over-broad edges cause collateral damage; an evaluation should report edge recall and precision independently of labelling accuracy. Writes should stay free. Anthropic's write-rule result and the design-rationale capture literature agree that forcing structure on the writer costs either gain or compliance, so decisions are best captured through the harness at the moment they are made (plan steps, branch and commit tools), which also preserves the alternatives that 63% of human ADRs omit.

**Compose notes extractively and keep derived views rebuildable.** The current-value view should be a deterministic projection, never an amendment chain handed to the model, since raw retrieval over chains of length 8 fell to 25.0% in P-Mem. A note composed by traversal and rendered as the ordered text of its nodes is traceable by construction; where a fluent summary is wanted, plan-then-write generation from selected nodes keeps uncited sentences near zero, but its AutoAIS of 72.8 to 89.3 means an entailment check is still needed ([Attribute First](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2403.17104)). Any derived summary should cite leaf evidence and be regenerated from source nodes, not from its own previous version: the Useful Memories ablation (append-only with the prior store hidden 70.0% against visible 50.0%) is the measured reason. Support counts or DRed-style maintenance tell the system which summaries to rebuild when a source is withdrawn.

**Evaluate on a benchmark that does not yet exist, and keep detection separate.** No published benchmark plants a wrong root decision with labelled dependents and independently supported items across sessions. The components exist: paired clean and faulty traces with recovery, recurrence, benign-preservation and replay-cost metrics (rollback repair); trap and control suites with rollback recall and downstream harm (MemTX); propagated-invalidation probes whose updates never name the dependent (STALE Type II); control twins that price false retraction (Bemula); success by dependency depth across sessions (MemoryArena [peer-reviewed, ICML 2026]) ([arXiv 2602.16313](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16313v1)). Assembled, they give a protocol in which a session-one decision is later shown wrong by an executable oracle, every later memory is labelled dependent, independent or unrelated, and the primary metric is the number of sessions until no dependent is delivered, alongside recurrence, benign preservation with and without the independence check, an edge-capture ablation including a laundering case, and results reported both with the root given and with the root to be detected. The cache claim needs its own controlled replay: immutable oldest-first nodes against the same information consolidated by rewriting, crossed with placement (system prefix against trailing message), at equal token count and at natural size, with gap distributions on both sides of the cache lifetime, isolated caches per run, and about 40 sessions per cell as in Lumer et al., reporting task quality alongside cost.

## Conclusion

The contribution space has shifted. The claim that no agent memory can withdraw a dependent subgraph is no longer safe in November 2026, because MemTX, PACMI and rollback repair all do it, and the classical literature shows the validity function they share is a solved, linear-time construction. What remains unclaimed is narrower and more defensible: a decision typed as a retraction root, a scope relation for "learned under" that is tested against independent support rather than treated as derivation, edges logged by the runtime rather than declared by the model, notes composed from immutable nodes rather than rewritten, and a delivery layer that withholds what the labelling withdraws. The novelty lives in capture and delivery, not in the reasoning engine, and the evaluation should be built around that: edge precision and recall, sessions until no dependent is delivered, and benign preservation are the numbers that would distinguish this design from its six nearest neighbours.

Two findings should change how the design is described. First, the prompt-cache argument for immutability holds across sessions and concurrent readers, not within a session, and it inverts at exactly the moment the design is most valuable: withdrawing a root whose dependents sit in the cached core should break the cache, because at a few cents per 100K tokens the cache is cheap and acting on a withdrawn root is not. Second, the design moves the system's weakest point rather than removing it. Model judgment no longer decides validity, but it still produces the nodes, the contradiction signals and, unless the harness logs them, the edges, and nothing in the design detects a wrong root earlier than the 14.2% step attribution and median step-7 onset the failure studies report. The design will be credible when it shows, on a planted-root benchmark, that withdrawal by recorded dependency stops dependents reaching the model faster than dreaming, deletion or reset, without discarding the knowledge that never depended on the root.
