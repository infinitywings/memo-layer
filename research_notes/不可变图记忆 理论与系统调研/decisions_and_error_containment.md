# Decisions and error containment in long-horizon LLM agents: propagation, decision capture, undo, persistence, and evaluation

Source tags used below: **[PR]** peer-reviewed venue; **[PRE]** arXiv preprint, no venue confirmed; **[VENDOR]** vendor post or documentation; **[CLASSIC]** pre-2024 source. "Not verified" means I did not confirm the item against the primary full text (for example it comes from a search-result summary, an abstract only, or a figure I could not read). "Carried over" means the number comes from this project's earlier notes (`research_notes/智能体记忆 验证门控 相关工作/`) and was not re-fetched in this pass. Research date: 2026-11-13.

## 1. Measurements of error propagation in long-horizon and multi-session agents

### Takeaway
In failed runs the decisive error is usually early and epistemic: in 1,184 failed CLI coding-agent runs it falls at median step 7 of 27, false premises are the largest trigger (30.7%), and 82% of failed runs keep executing without progress. In a 95-run multi-agent study the deviated state was retained and reused in 74 runs, and replanning never removed it. Stored errors also compound across tasks (add-all memory scores 3.7 to 12 points below a frozen memory), but most of the trajectory-level evidence is 2025 to 2026 preprints, and none of it measures propagation from a wrong *decision* through a persistent memory across sessions.

### Cited Findings

**Single-agent trajectories (coding and tool use)**
- **"Failure as a Process: An Anatomy of CLI Coding Agent Trajectories"** [PRE, Jul 2026; Zhao et al.]. Manual annotation of 1,794 valid Terminal-Bench trajectories (610 successful, 1,184 failed; 89 tasks; 3 scaffolds × 7 models; over 63,000 steps). Decisive errors: 57.9% epistemic, 32.8% competence, 9.4% environment; epistemic errors are 44% to 80% of failures in every system. False premises are the largest single trigger (30.7% of decisive errors). Median decisive error at step 7 (mean 11.92) while failed runs have a median of 27 steps. Median recovery window (lock-in minus decisive error) is 1 step; observable signals appear about 10 steps after the decisive error. 82% of failed runs keep executing without progress. The wrong-problem-repair pattern (the agent fixes something other than the real cause) covers 24% of failed runs and 39% of wasted execution; the same-approach-repetition pattern covers 15% and 29%. Failed runs respond to at least one error signal before lock-in 37% of the time versus 92% for successful runs. 26% of failed runs fabricate success. A real-time prefix monitor flags only 3.7 to 8.7% of failures before lock-in (best recall 28.8%). — [arXiv HTML 2607.09510](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09510)
- **AgentDebug, "Where LLM Agents Fail and How They Can Learn from Failures"** [PRE, Sep 2025; Zhu et al.]. Taxonomy over memory, reflection, planning, action and system errors; AgentErrorBench has 200 annotated failed trajectories (100 ALFWorld, 50 WebShop, 50 GAIA; Cohen's κ = 0.55). The paper names error propagation as the main bottleneck for agent reliability, with memory and reflection errors the most common propagation sources, usually in steps 5 to 15. Isolating the earliest critical error: all-correct accuracy 24.3% versus 0.3% for the best baseline; step accuracy 45.0% versus 28.0%; corrective feedback gives up to 26% relative task-success gain (ALFWorld: 21 to 55 for GPT-4o-mini). The page gives no share of failures with an early root cause. — [arXiv HTML 2509.25370](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25370)
- **SLUMP, "When the Specification Emerges"** [PR, Findings of EMNLP 2026; Yan, Chen, Zhang]. 20 ML papers, 371 atomic verifiable components, about 60 coding requests per paper; design commitments are disclosed progressively. A single-shot control with the full specification has higher implementation fidelity on 16/20 papers (Claude Code) and 14/20 (Codex). Abstract only; no overall loss percentage given. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.76/)

**Multi-agent failure taxonomies and attribution**
- **MAST, "Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?"** [arXiv 2503.13657 v3, Oct 2025; Cemri et al., UC Berkeley; the arXiv page names no venue, a NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks acceptance is not verified]. 1,642 annotated traces from 7 frameworks; 14 failure modes in 3 categories; human κ = 0.88; LLM judge (o1, few-shot) accuracy 0.94, κ = 0.77. Reported failure rates of the systems: 41% to 86.7%. Mode shares: system design FM-1.1 11.8%, FM-1.2 1.5%, FM-1.3 15.7%, FM-1.4 2.80%, FM-1.5 12.4%; inter-agent misalignment FM-2.1 2.20%, FM-2.2 6.80%, FM-2.3 7.40%, FM-2.4 0.85%, FM-2.5 1.90%, FM-2.6 13.2%; task verification FM-3.1 6.20%, FM-3.2 8.20%, FM-3.3 9.10%. Summing gives about 44.2% / 32.4% / 23.5% by category (my sums; the paper text does not state category totals). Two targeted interventions on ChatDev raised task success by only +9.4% and +15.6%. MAST is a mode taxonomy, not a propagation measurement. — [arXiv HTML 2503.13657v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3)
- **Who&When, "Which Agent Causes Task Failures and When?"** [arXiv 2505.00212; Zhang et al.; page says "camera-ready", ICML 2025 not verified]. Failure logs from 127 multi-agent systems. Best automated method: 53.5% accuracy at naming the responsible agent, 14.2% at the decisive error step. — [arXiv 2505.00212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00212)
- **"Dependable Long-Horizon LLM Agents: A Trajectory-Centered Empirical Study"** [PRE, Nov 2026; Wang et al., Villanova]. 95 failed runs from Who&When and MAD, annotated by an LLM under a written protocol (no human inter-annotator agreement reported). Fault origin by phase: dispatch/execute 34 (about 36%), deliberate/decompose 24 (25%), admission 13 (14%), observe/integrate 10, verify 9, recover 4, terminate 1. By kind: decisions made with adequate information 42%, fabrications 26%, misintegrated observations 11%. On Who&When the origin sits at a median 0.18 of run length; the origin is earlier than the human-labelled "decisive step" in 37 of 88 runs (median 3 steps earlier). Contamination (deviated state retained and reused) appears in 74 of 95 runs; in 85 runs the deviated state crossed two or more state domains; cascade in 23. In 68 of 95 runs (72%) an observation contradicting the erroneous belief was present before the symptom, and in 60 of those 68 (about 88%) it was never mentioned (41) or acknowledged and ignored (19). Something flagged a deviation in 29 runs; a response followed in 34 runs; none removed the deviated state and 4 removed part of it. Replans often changed the next action while keeping the belief behind the loop. All control "reach" figures are judgments from traces, not interventions. — [arXiv HTML 2611.13254](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13254)

**Memory-induced error propagation**
- **Xiong et al., "How Memory Management Impacts LLM Agents: An Empirical Study of Experience-Following Behavior"** [arXiv 2505.16067 v2; listed as an ACL 2026 long paper in the ACL Anthology preview, which I did not open]. Agents reproduce retrieved records when the new input is similar (Pearson r ≈ 1 between input and output similarity for RegAgent with a large memory). Storing every execution ("add-all") scores below a frozen memory on all four agents: RegAgent 55.48 vs 67.53, EHRAgent 13.05 vs 16.75, AgentDriver 32.32 vs 40.11, CIC-IoT 59.90 vs 71.50. A strict (human-level) write evaluator gives 70.95 / 38.50 / 51.00 / 85.40. Some coarse LLM evaluators fall below the frozen baseline (C1 63.18 vs 67.53 on RegAgent). History-based deletion with a strict evaluator: EHRAgent 42.06, AgentDriver 51.81, CIC-IoT 89.60. The paper gives no count of propagated errors; the gap to an error-free memory is shown only in figures. — [arXiv HTML 2505.16067v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067v2); [ACL Anthology preview listing](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.acl-long.27/)
- **MemoryArena** [PR, ICML 2026 (PMLR v306); He et al.]. 766 tasks (the per-domain counts in the fetched text sum to 736), 6.9 interdependent subtasks and 57 action steps per task on average; later sessions depend on results of earlier ones. Average task success: long-context 0.16, external memory systems 0.12 to 0.15, RAG up to 0.23. Success at subtask depth k decays for every method with no flat region, which the authors call "belief drift" (per-depth values only in a figure). — [arXiv HTML 2602.16313](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16313v1); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/he26am.html)
- **CL-Bench** [PRE, Jun 2026]. The authors report that accumulated state often hurts instead of helping, which they attribute to spurious generalizations, stale beliefs, over-reliance on recent instances and rigidity in early wrong beliefs. Carried over. — [arXiv 2606.05661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05661)
- **"Honest Lying" (memory confabulation)** [workshop, ICML 2026 "Failure Modes in Agentic AI"]. In 16 ALFWorld environments where a Reflexion-style agent never succeeded, 0 of 121 reflections named the correct target object; Reflection Repetition Rate 0.64, lowered to 0.10 by programmatic extraction of failure signals. Carried over. — [arXiv 2605.29463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29463)

**Self-conditioning on errors in long contexts**
- **Sinha et al., "The Illusion of Diminishing Returns: Measuring Long Horizon Execution in LLMs"** [arXiv 2509.09677 v3, Mar 2026; an ICLR 2026 poster listing exists, not opened]. Injecting errors into the model's own history lowers accuracy at turn 100 further as the injected error rate rises, beyond the long-context effect; the authors state that scaling model size alone does not reduce it. Qwen3 thinking models do not self-condition (turn-100 accuracy stable regardless of error rate). A sliding window over recent turns helps, but only for Markovian tasks. Exact per-model accuracies are in Figures 5 and 6 and were not readable: not verified. — [arXiv HTML 2509.09677v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09677v3); [ICLR 2026 listing](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10011614)

### Inferences
- The numbers that bear most directly on the design claim are: early onset (median step 7 of 27; origin at 0.18 of run length), late visibility (about 10 steps), and retained-and-reused deviated state (74/95 runs). Together they describe a wrong root that many later steps are built on before anyone notices.
- A large share of roots are premises or decisions rather than execution slips (false premises 30.7% of decisive errors; wrong decisions made despite adequate information 42% of origins; 56/95 runs where the fault sat in a plan, brief or stop rule; see section 3). This supports treating the chosen route as the unit to record and retract.
- Detection is the weaker link, not removal. Agents ignore contradicting evidence (about 88% of evidence-bearing runs) and automated step-level attribution is 14.2%. A tracing mechanism only helps after something declares the root wrong.
- Experience-following shows the multi-session version: a wrong record is copied when retrieved and re-stored, so harm grows with the number of similar later tasks unless writes are strictly evaluated.

### Gaps
- No study found measures propagation from a wrong high-level decision through a persistent memory across sessions with counted dependents; the trajectory studies are single-run and the memory studies concern experiences or facts.
- Exact self-conditioning numbers (turn-100 accuracy by injected error rate) could not be read. Secondary claims that GPT-5 thinking executes over 2,100 steps versus 432 for Claude 4 Sonnet come from a search summary of v1: not verified.
- Venues for MAST and Who&When were not confirmed on primary pages. The 95-run study uses LLM annotation only.

## 2. Decision capture: classical rationale methods and LLM-agent systems that record plans, decisions or rationales

### Takeaway
Classical practice records a decision as an immutable, typed unit with context, alternatives and consequences, and supersedes rather than deletes; its chronic problem is capture cost, and even today about 63% of open-source ADRs are created already "accepted". Among LLM-agent systems, 2025 to 2026 work records branches with purpose (GCC), derivation DAGs between beliefs (MemTX), typed provenance graphs (rollback repair) and immutable traces (MemTrace), but I found none that types "decision" as a node and links later knowledge to the decision in force. Runtime-logged edges are the most reliable capture channel; model post-hoc extraction reaches decision-level F1 of about 0.66 and self-declared parentage fails in the cases that test it.

### Cited Findings

**Classical decision records and design rationale**
- **Nygard, "Documenting Architecture Decisions"** [CLASSIC, practitioner blog, Nov 2011]. An ADR has Title, Context (forces, stated neutrally), Decision ("We will ..."), Status (proposed, accepted; later deprecated or superseded with a reference to the replacement) and Consequences (all of them: positive, negative, neutral). Reversed decisions are kept and marked superseded. Motivation: without recorded rationale a newcomer can only blindly accept or blindly reverse a past decision, and ADRs exist to make both avoidable. — [Cognitect blog](https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions)
- **IBIS, PHI, QOC, DRL** [CLASSIC; summarized from a tertiary source]. IBIS (Kunz and Rittel, 1970): issues, positions, arguments. gIBIS (1987): graphical IBIS. PHI (McCall, 1978): adds sub-issue hierarchy. QOC (MacLean et al.): questions, options, criteria, with assessments linking options to criteria. DRL (Lee, 1991): decision problems, alternatives, goals, claims. Capture approaches and trade-offs: reconstruction (non-disruptive but costly and biased), record-and-replay, methodological byproduct (low cost, schema hard to design), apprentice, automatic generation from execution history (consistent and current, expensive to compile). Formal schemas make capture more intrusive; informal records are cheap but hard for machines to interpret. — [Wikipedia, Design rationale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_rationale)
- **Shipman and Marshall, "Formality Considered Harmful"** [CLASSIC, CSCW journal 1999]. Argues from experience that systems forcing users to categorize content into a formalism, design-rationale systems among them, impose effort that users resist. From a search-result summary: not verified against the paper. — [author copy](https://people.engr.tamu.edu/shipman/formality-paper/harmful.html)
- **Buckingham Shum et al., "Hypermedia support for argumentation-based rationale: 15 years on from gIBIS and QOC"** [CLASSIC, about 2005]. Names the "DR capture problem": getting quality input without disrupting the process or employing dedicated documenters. From a search-result summary: not verified. — [AcaWiki summary](https://acawiki.org/Hypermedia_support_for_argumentation-based_rationale:_15_Years_on_from_gIBIS_and_QOC)

**How ADRs are used in practice**
- **"Architecture Decision Records: Adoption, Impact, and Developer Engagement in Open-Source Software"** [PR, ICSA 2026; Santana Junior et al.]. 921 GitHub repositories, 5,800 ADRs. About 3,674 (63%) are opened directly with status "accepted" or equivalent, which the authors say bypasses the deliberation ADRs are meant to capture. Correlations with code-quality metrics are mostly small. Abstract only; no supersession rates. — [ICSA 2026 programme](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icsa-2026/icsa-2026-papers/34/Architecture-Decision-Records-Adoption-Impact-and-Developer-Engagement-in-Open-Sou)
- **Buchgeher et al., MSR study of ADRs on GitHub** [PR, about 2023]. Adoption is low but rising; in roughly half the repositories usage stops at 1 to 5 records. From a search-result summary: not verified. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371709784_Using_Architecture_Decision_Records_in_Open_Source_Projects-An_MSR_Study_on_GitHub)

**Model-based extraction of decisions and rationale (post-hoc)**
- **Zhou et al., "Using LLMs in Generating Design Rationale for Software Architecture Decisions"** [arXiv 2504.20781; ACM DOI 10.1145/3785010, journal not verified]. Against expert ground truth, LLM-generated rationale has precision 0.267 to 0.278, recall 0.627 to 0.715, F1 0.351 to 0.389; LLMs give 4.55 to 5.77 arguments per decision versus 1.98 for experts. From a search-result summary: not verified. — [arXiv 2504.20781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20781)
- **Dhar et al., "Can LLMs Generate Architectural Design Decisions?"** [arXiv 2403.01709, 2024; venue not verified]. GPT-4 zero-shot generates relevant decisions from ADR context but falls short of human level. Search summary only. — [arXiv HTML 2403.01709](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.01709)
- **AssumptionMiner** [PRE, Jul 2026; single author]. After code generation, an LLM lists design choices the code makes beyond the prompt as typed records (category, rationale, alternatives, confidence, severity), links each to code regions, and regenerates only linked regions when one is revised. 180 tasks, 676 assumptions; every task had at least two implicit assumptions (mean 3.8). Extraction F1: 0.816 at category level, 0.66 at decision level for the best open configuration (the paper says this level remains unsolved); localization threshold accuracy 56.9% to 64.2%. Regeneration keeps edit distance at 0.244 versus 0.549 for full regeneration, but syntactic validity drops to 83.3%; cascading revisions are acknowledged and not handled. Reference labels were LLM-derived. — [arXiv HTML 2607.22898](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22898)
- **ChatGPT memory audit** [PR, WWW 2026]. 84% of stored memories are directly grounded in what the user said; 96% are system-initiated. Carried over. — [arXiv 2602.01450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01450)
- **"Manufactured Confidence"** [PRE, Jun 2026]. Consolidation in the style of mem0 or LangMem rewrites a hedged remark into a confident dated fact that agents then treat as verified. Carried over. — [arXiv 2606.29279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29279)

**Agent systems that record plans, branches, beliefs or traces as first-class state**
- **Git Context Controller (GCC)** [PRE; arXiv 2508.00031 v2, Mar 2026; Wu et al.]. Detection: explicit agent-invoked commands. COMMIT appends Branch Purpose, Previous Progress Summary and This Commit's Contribution; BRANCH opens an isolated trajectory and records its intent and motivation; MERGE records rationale and outcome; CONTEXT retrieves at several granularities. SWE-Bench Verified ablation (Claude 4 Sonnet): raw 67.2%, + roadmap and COMMIT 69.1%, + logs and CONTEXT 75.3%, + metadata 77.8%, + BRANCH and MERGE 80.2%. SWE-Bench Lite 48.00% with Claude 3.5 Sonnet. The paper describes no procedure for an abandoned branch or for undoing a wrong decision, and its headline gain figures are internally inconsistent (abstract "over 13%", introduction "over 24%"). — [arXiv HTML 2508.00031v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00031v2)
- **MemTX, "Transactional Belief Commit for Stateful Agent Memory"** [PRE, Jul 2026, "under review"; Li et al.]. Writing a memory and committing a belief are separate steps: records carry evidence, permissions, provenance and validity, pass an eight-state lifecycle, and are linked by a derivation DAG (derived-from edges). Stated limitation: repair reaches only derivations that were recorded; in the "source-scope laundering" case, where the agent transcribes content without declaring its parent, every backbone fails every case. Results are in section 3. — [arXiv HTML 2607.23929v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23929v2)
- **Dependency-guided rollback repair** [PRE, Aug 2026; Yu et al.]. Detection: runtime instrumentation. A typed graph with user inputs, execution steps (memory reads, claims, plans, tool actions, observations, answers, memory mutations) and memory records; edges (cite, support, produce, delete, update, consolidate, supersede, derive) are set deterministically from logged fields, not inferred from text. Execution-order ("chain") edges are excluded from propagation because a later step is not evidence of contamination. Limitation: missing provenance under-traces contamination; spurious edges cause unnecessary invalidation. — [arXiv HTML 2608.10502](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10502v1)
- **MemTrace** [PRE, Oct 2026; Xu et al.]. Completed units of coding work are sealed as immutable Memory Traces bound to files, symbols and tests, linked by depend, verify, correct and supersede edges; a later timestamp by itself is not treated as supersession. It has no decision or rationale node type and does not describe invalidation cascading along edges. DeepSWE pass@1 56.6% vs 35.4% native (Codex CLI); ablation: trace store only 32.1%, + trace graph 44.2%, full 56.6%. — [arXiv HTML 2610.04838](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04838v1)
- **Agent Workflow Memory** [PR, ICML 2025; Wang, Mao, Fried, Neubig]. Detection: post-hoc induction by a model from successful trajectories. Stores reusable workflows (procedures, not decisions with rationale). +24.6% and +51.1% relative success on Mind2Web and WebArena. From a search-result summary of the proceedings page: not verified against the paper. — [PMLR v267](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wang25bx.html)
- **PROV-AGENT** [PR, IEEE eScience 2025; Souza et al., ORNL]. Extends W3C PROV with agent, prompt/response, model-invocation and tool entities, captured via MCP instrumentation into one provenance graph for scientific workflows. No accuracy numbers found. Search summary only. — [arXiv 2508.02866](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.02866)
- **ProjectGuard (in SLUMP)** [PR, Findings of EMNLP 2026]. Keeping external semantic and structural project state recovers 90% of Claude Code's measured faithfulness gap under progressive specification. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.76/)
- **PoS, "Beyond Memory: Harnessing Long-Horizon Agents with Explicit Belief States"** [PRE, Oct 2026]. Maintains an explicit belief (world-state estimate plus unresolved requirements), validates its consistency, and detects "Belief Trapping" (acting without progress). Abstract only; no numbers. — [arXiv 2610.01415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01415)
- **Anthropic, "The components of agent memory"** [VENDOR, 6 Nov 2026]. Stronger models chose to store plans and justified rules: Fable 5's `global.md` held the current goal, a prewritten gym plan and memory rules; Opus 5's entries held a rule plus how it knew the rule was true. A fixed failure-only recording rule cut the memory gain by 0.234 (Opus 5). Links between wiki pages are navigational; the post describes no dependency edges between a plan and notes learned under it. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)
- **Anthropic dreaming cookbook** [VENDOR, Nov 2026]. Contradiction policy includes that a single session's passing remark never overrides a recorded decision. Carried over. — [Cookbook](https://platform.claude.com/cookbook/managed-agents-cma-dreaming-with-a-dynamic-workflow)

**Evidence on whether agents reliably use an explicit channel**
- Sonnet 5 read memory before answering about 9 times in 10 unprompted; Sonnet 4.6 less than half the time [VENDOR]. Carried over. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)
- With a reachable provenance link and a budget of 2 inspections out of 6 records, 16 models inspected the critical provenance path in about 17% of episodes [PRE, Aug 2026]. Carried over. — [arXiv 2608.25553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25553)

### Inferences
- Reliability ranking of detection channels, from the evidence above: (1) runtime-logged events and edges are deterministic for whatever passes through the harness (rollback repair, PROV-AGENT, MemTrace bindings); (2) verbatim user instructions are mostly grounded (84%); (3) explicit agent tool calls record purpose well when made (GCC), but agents skip optional steps (17% provenance inspection; under half memory reads on a weaker model) and do not declare parentage (MemTX laundering case fails every time); (4) model post-hoc extraction is high-recall and low-precision for rationale (recall 0.63 to 0.72, precision about 0.27) and about 0.66 F1 at decision level; (5) self-diagnosis of what went wrong is the weakest (0/121 correct reflections; 14.2% step attribution).
- Humans mostly record decisions after making them (63% of ADRs born "accepted"), so the alternatives and context that make a later retraction traceable are the part most often missing. An agent memory that wants alternatives and consequences has to capture them at decision time through the harness, not rely on later reconstruction.
- Nygard's supersede-not-delete rule and the versioning used by MemTrace and ERRAND (write a new version, keep the old) are the same immutability stance the design takes. What the classical formats lack is machine-followable links from a decision to what was later learned under it; "Consequences" is free text.
- The closest published structures to "decision node plus learned-under edges" are GCC branches (purpose recorded, no retraction semantics) and derivation DAGs (MemTX, rollback repair: retraction semantics, no decision type). Combining them appears to be open ground, with the caveat that I ran a limited number of searches.

### Gaps
- I found no source on research-agent lab notebooks or provenance-linked research knowledge bases that type decisions and measure anything; one search returned only PROV-AGENT.
- No measurement found of how often an agent, given a "record decision" tool, actually calls it at the right moments, or of plan steps used as decision markers.
- IBIS, QOC, DRL and the capture-problem literature are cited through tertiary or summary pages; the original papers were not opened.
- "Decision trace" as a named mechanism: no primary source found.

## 3. Containment and undo: rollback, replanning, forgetting, retraction and ripple effects

### Takeaway
Checkpoint and rollback restore files, sandboxes or context, but not beliefs: in a 95-run study a restore would have sufficed in 35 runs while in 56 the fault sat in a plan, brief or stop rule that a restore would replay. Removing a bad memory is also not recovery: two 2026 preprints show that tracing derivation edges from the faulty root recovers more (0.853 vs 0.340 for deleting retrieved memories; cascading rollback worth about 11 points) and preserves benign state, yet leaves residual recurrence (0.266) and repairs only recorded edges. Knowledge-editing work gives the same lesson at the parametric level: edits succeed locally (edit success 88 to 100%) while dependent multi-hop answers collapse to 5 to 8%.

### Cited Findings

**Checkpoint and rollback of environment and context**
- **Claude Code checkpointing** [VENDOR docs]. A checkpoint is taken before each user prompt; snapshots are kept for the 100 most recent checkpoints and deleted about 30 days after the session's last save. `/rewind` can restore code, conversation, or both, or summarize from a point. Not tracked: files changed only by Bash commands, most subagent edits, external and concurrent-session edits, symlinked or hard-linked paths. The docs position checkpoints as fast recovery within a session, not a replacement for version control; the page describes file and conversation state only and says nothing about memory or what was learned after the checkpoint. — [Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/checkpointing)
- **AgentRewind** [PRE, Aug 2026]. Records aligned checkpoints of agent context and controlled environment so execution can resume from an earlier point using information from previous attempts; introduces MettleBench. Abstract gives no numbers. — [arXiv 2608.14380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14380)
- **Rollback-Induced Reflection** [PRE, Sep 2026]. Restores a selected prior state while carrying forward distilled lessons from the abandoned trajectory; frames recovery as when to intervene, where to resume, what to keep. Abstract gives no numbers. — [arXiv 2609.18304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18304)
- **DeltaBox, SetupX, "Safe to Resume?"** [PRE, 2026]. Millisecond sandbox checkpoint and rollback; a LIFO stack of container snapshots under agent control; and an argument that correct restoration can still break the dependencies that justify security-relevant state. Search summaries only: not verified. — [DeltaBox](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22781v1); [SetupX](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.26186); [Safe to Resume?](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29381)
- **Limits measured on traces** [PRE, Nov 2026]. Restoring the agent's own state before the fault origin would have sufficed in 35 of 95 failed runs; in 56 the fault sat in a plan, brief or stop rule that a restore would replay. Checkpoint/restore was the primary applicable control in 0 runs. — [arXiv HTML 2611.13254](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13254)

**Branch-and-backtrack search**
- **LATS** [arXiv 2310.04406; ICML 2024 per general knowledge, venue not shown on the arXiv page]. Monte Carlo tree search over agent actions with LM value functions, self-reflection and environment feedback: 92.7% pass@1 on HumanEval (GPT-4), average score 75.9 on WebShop (GPT-3.5). — [arXiv 2310.04406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04406)
- **SWE-Search** [PR, ICLR 2025]. MCTS plus iterative refinement so software agents can backtrack: 23% relative improvement across five models on SWE-bench over agents without MCTS. Abstract-level, via search summary. — [ICLR 2025 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/a1e6783e4d739196cad3336f12d402bf-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **GCC** [PRE]. BRANCH and MERGE add 2.4 points on SWE-Bench Verified (77.8% to 80.2%). — [arXiv HTML 2508.00031v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00031v2)

**Plan repair and replanning**
- In the 95-run study a response (usually replan or retry) followed in 34 runs; none removed the deviated state [PRE]. — [arXiv HTML 2611.13254](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13254)
- In CLI coding runs, repairing something other than the real cause accounts for 24% of failed runs and 39% of wasted execution; successful recoveries take a median 5 steps versus 12 for failed ones [PRE]. — [arXiv HTML 2607.09510](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09510)

**Memory repair that follows dependencies**
- **"From Faulty Memories to Corrected Actions: Dependency-Guided Rollback Repair"** [PRE, Aug 2026]. Claim: "fault removal is not state recovery"; deleting a faulty source leaves propagated claims, actions and derived memories active. Method: from each diagnosed fault, follow derivation edges to a raw affected set; keep any reachable node that has a sufficient admissible source outside the faulty and affected sets (independent-support check; preserved nodes may not validate others); delete faults, quarantine unsupported affected memories, invalidate unsupported steps, replay only what still reaches the final answer. Controlled benchmark (150 tool-use cases; faults: 42 poisoned, 34 stale, 34 wrong-user, 40 summary-drift). Recovery 0.853 vs LLM-judge repair 0.773, AgentTrace-style 0.607, full memory reset 0.407, delete retrieved memories 0.340, MemAudit-style 0.320. Benign preservation 1.000 (full reset 0.000; delete-retrieved 0.789). Recurrence 0.266 (LLM-judge repair 0.121). Claim-invalidation F1 0.566 (LLM judge 0.946). 43.3% fewer replayed steps and 41.8% fewer LLM calls than LLM-judge repair. Ablations: no rollback planner, recovery 0.713 and recurrence 0.430; no support check, recovery 0.880 but benign preservation 0.986 and more replay; no selective replay, recurrence 0.071 at replay ratio 0.755. Transfer to an adapted LongMemEval-V2 (50 cases): recovery 0.680. Diagnosis is assumed given; external side effects are not undone; single runs; benchmark by the same authors. — [arXiv HTML 2608.10502](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10502v1)
- **MemTX** [PRE, Jul 2026]. On retraction, typed cascading repair walks descendants in the derivation DAG: beliefs revoked, summaries and profiles quarantined for rebuild, tool actions compensated or logged as leaked; irreversible tool calls are gated on tentative state. Main suite (90 cases: 60 traps, 30 controls) task success vs best baseline: 0.956 vs 0.822 (Qwen3-8B), 0.944 vs 0.833 (GLM-4.7-Flash), 0.922 vs 0.811 (GPT-5.4-mini), 0.756 vs 0.778 (GPT-5.5, a tie). Rollback recall 1.000 on four backbones versus 0.833 for baselines without repair. Removing cascading rollback costs about 11 points of task success. Zero downstream harm in 24 refund episodes (baselines about 0.17 to 0.5). When a polluted result drives an irreversible action within one staging window, success is 0.60. Self-built benchmarks and mock tools. — [arXiv HTML 2607.23929v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23929v2)
- **MemSecBench** [PRE, Jul 2026]. Write–Execute–Forget protocol, 310 cases, 24 configurations. Malicious memory persists in 84.2% of cases; 50.3% complete the full write-to-execute chain; selective repair succeeds in 56.1% of successfully poisoned cases. Removal passes in 86.3% but benign memory is fully preserved in only 62.5% (these two from a search summary and earlier notes). — [arXiv 2607.27080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27080)
- **MemAudit** [PRE, May 2026]. Leave-one-out replay: remove one memory entry, replay the query, score the drop in harm; fused with a memory-consistency graph. MINJA attack success 50 to 83% falls to 0 to 10%; breaks down above about 20 to 25% contamination. Carried over. — [arXiv HTML 2605.23723](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23723)
- **TEPA** [PRE, Aug 2026]. Fresh evidence contradicting a precedent under the same key revokes it; revoked records are kept and re-promotable. 0.950 under controlled drift versus 0.210 for append-only and last-write-wins. Carried over. — [arXiv 2608.07429](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07429)
- **Anthropic Dreams** [VENDOR docs, research preview]. A dream reads a store plus 1 to 100 past sessions and writes a new, separate store with stale or contradicted entries replaced; the input store is never modified. Carried over. — [Dreams docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/dreams)
- **ERRAND** [PRE, Sep 2026]. Treats rechecking a stored item as an action competing with the task; repairs write a new version and never delete. Beats eager revalidation by 10.0 points at the base cap; uncapped it spends 11.0% of steps on rechecks versus 70.7% for eager revalidation. Abstract only. — [arXiv 2609.29545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29545)

**Selective forgetting and memory unlearning**
- **Bemula, "Can Agents Forget When Asked?"** [PRE, Oct 2026; Tsinghua]. 50 multi-session dialogues (forget and control versions), 135 forget requests of six types (factual, compositional, semantic, inferential, temporal, replacement), 1,742 questions; suites for retention, efficacy, preservation (over-deletion), relearning and extraction attacks. No system passes more than 1.5% of forget requests end to end. Forgetting efficacy is mostly below 25%; attack robustness is at most 35.7 for any system. Causes named: no removal path (append-only stores), overwrite without deletion, and redundant copies of the same information as raw turns, extracted facts and compressed notes, so removing one copy leaves the rest. LangMem deleted facts no request targeted; Full Context over-refused (66 of 81 preservation failures). Only 13 inferential requests; derived summaries and graph neighbours are not analysed. Synthetic dialogues. — [arXiv HTML 2610.18052](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18052v1)
- **AUBench** [PRE, Oct 2026]. Agent unlearning must cover parametric knowledge, persistent memory and tool-call reacquisition; parameter-level unlearning alone is insufficient because forgotten information persists in memory artifacts or is reacquired by tools. Abstract gives no numbers. — [arXiv 2610.17659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17659)
- **MemoryAgentBench** [ICLR 2026 per earlier notes, not verified]. Multi-hop conflict resolution at most about 7% for all methods; a critique finds that a frozen newest-statement-wins rule answers 80.25% of the conflict split. Carried over. — [arXiv HTML 2507.05257](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05257v1); [critique listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/169013)

**Knowledge-editing ripple effects (propagating a change to dependents)**
- **RippleEdits** [PR, TACL 2024; Cohen et al.]. 5K factual edits with criteria for implied changes to related facts. Prominent parametric editing methods fail to make consistent changes; a simple in-context editing baseline scores best. — [arXiv 2307.12976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12976)
- **MQuAKE** [PR, EMNLP 2023; CLASSIC by date; Zhong et al.]. 9,218 counterfactual and 1,868 temporal instances. GPT-J on MQuAKE-CF: base multi-hop accuracy 40.5%; after editing, ROME has 88.3% edit-wise success but 7.4% multi-hop, MEMIT 96.2% and 7.0%, MEND 73.2% and 8.0%, fine-tuning 46.9% and 1.5%. Vicuna-7B: 30.2% base to 4.9% (MEMIT). With more simultaneous edits accuracy falls further (MEMIT 11.8 to 5.4 from 1 to 3,000 edited instances). The memory-based MeLLo, which keeps edits external and checks each sub-answer against them, reaches 38.9 at k=1 and 14.2 at k=3,000 (GPT-J) and 52.1 to 30.9 with GPT-3.5; its retrieval accuracy drops from 88.1 to 59.7 as edits accumulate. — [arXiv HTML 2305.14795v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14795v3)
- **Successors** [PR]. ChainEdit (ACL 2025) raises RippleEdits logical generalization from 19.3% to 33.1% by adding rule-derived chains of related facts; EchoEdit (AAAI-26) uses a graph-guided planner to control ripple effects on MQuAKE and RippleEdits. Search summaries: not verified. — [ChainEdit](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08427); [EchoEdit](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/39013)

**Retraction in knowledge bases (classical)**
- **Doyle, "A Truth Maintenance System"** [CLASSIC, Artificial Intelligence 12(3), 1979]. Beliefs carry recorded justifications and are labelled IN or OUT; dependency-directed backtracking changes the current assumption set when a contradiction is found. Page range differs between catalogues. Search summary only. — [AITopics entry](https://aitopics.org/doc/classics:CA80645E); [Wikipedia, Reason maintenance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reason_maintenance)
- **Delete and Rederive (DRed)** [CLASSIC]. For incremental maintenance of derived facts: first over-delete everything that depends, possibly indirectly, on a deleted fact, then rederive what still holds from remaining facts. Described in a 2019 Oxford paper; the counting alternative (tracking the number of derivations per derived fact) is from Gupta, Mumick and Subrahmanian, SIGMOD 1993. Attribution of DRed to the 1993 paper not verified. — [Motik et al. 2019](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/boris.motik/pubs/mnph19maintenance-revisited.pdf); [SIGMOD 1993 record](https://researchr.org/publication/GuptaMS93)
- **NeuSymMS** [PRE, May 2026]. An LLM extracts facts and a CLIPS rule engine retracts stale facts with Doyle-style justification tracking. Search summary only. — [arXiv HTML 2605.17596](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17596v1)

### Inferences
- The design claim has direct, though preprint-only, support: both dependency-following systems beat root-only removal and full reset, and removing the cascade costs about 11 points (MemTX) or drops recovery to 0.713 (rollback repair without the planner).
- The same papers bound the claim in four ways. (1) Only recorded edges are repaired; undeclared derivation defeats the trace (laundering case fails on every backbone). (2) Residual influence remains after graph repair (recurrence 0.266), and an LLM judge finds affected claims better (F1 0.946 vs 0.566), so graph tracing and model judgment are complementary. (3) The root is assumed already diagnosed. (4) External side effects are logged or compensated, not undone.
- Edge semantics matter for over-pruning. The design's edge is "learned while the decision was in force", which is a scope or temporal relation. Rollback repair deliberately excludes execution-order edges from propagation, and MemTrace refuses to treat temporal order as supersession. An in-force edge will therefore mark independently true knowledge as dependent unless a second step checks for support outside the retracted set. That step is exactly DRed's "rederive", the counting algorithm's alternative-derivation count, and the rollback-repair independent-support check (which changed benign preservation from 0.986 to 1.000).
- Checkpoint/rollback and memory retraction address different state. Rollback returns files and context to a point in time and discards everything after it, true or not; route-level pruning keeps time moving and withdraws by dependency. The 35-versus-56 split suggests rollback alone is insufficient when the wrong item is the plan itself.
- Knowledge-editing results argue for keeping decisions and their dependents as external, explicit records: in-context or memory-based edits propagate to dependents better than weight edits, and still degrade as the number of edits and retrieval misses grow.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed evaluation found of dependency-directed memory retraction in LLM agents; MemTX and rollback repair are 2026 preprints evaluated on their authors' own benchmarks.
- Numbers for AgentRewind, Rollback-Induced Reflection, DeltaBox and SetupX were not obtained (abstracts or search summaries only).
- I found no unlearning benchmark that removes a memory together with its derived summaries and measures the derivatives; Bemula has 13 inferential requests and does not analyse derived summaries.
- No source found on git-based rollback of an agent's memory store with measured outcomes (Letta's git-backed MemFS is documented but not evaluated, per earlier notes).
- RippleEdits' six criteria and per-method scores were not extracted (abstract only).

## 4. How long a wrong note or belief persists once written, and what currently removes it

### Takeaway
Once written, a wrong or superseded memory generally stays until something external intervenes: poisoned entries persist in 84.2% of cases and injected goals survive 100 unrelated sessions; agents act on a superseded constraint in about 75 to 77% of decisions while inspecting its provenance in about 17% of episodes; and no mainstream memory system passes more than 1.5% of explicit forget requests. The removal mechanisms with any measured effect are a model rereading transcripts (one vendor case study), explicit user correction (91.49% of visible resolutions in coding sessions), same-key overwrite (works for single-hop updates only), and forced verification (+61 to +81 points). I found no duration-to-removal measurement in sessions or days for honest (non-adversarial) wrong notes.

### Cited Findings

**Case evidence**
- **Anthropic, Pokémon Red, Mt. Moon** [VENDOR, single case]. Claude wrote that the PokéCenter landmark was on the east side of the cave; it is on the west. Trusting the note, it marked the cave as done, walked off a one-way ledge back onto the earlier route three times, then wrote that it would stop instead of continuing to loop; an automatic reply told it to continue and it walked to Pewter City. An offline dreaming pass checked the note against the session transcript and rewrote it. Over 3,000 cumulative actions: corrected memory reached Cerulean City at action 1,935; uncorrected memory never got past the mountain; no memory never reached Cerulean. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)
- Same post, on always-loaded memory: a wrong note in the injected top layer travels into every session. Stated open problems: continuously checking all memories may be impractical, dreaming costs compute, memory drifts as the environment changes, and keeping memory reliably current is unresolved; suggested but untested directions are focusing checks on volatile information or tuning dreaming cadence by access frequency. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)

**Persistence rates**
- **MemSecBench** [PRE]. Malicious memory persists in 84.2% of 310 cases; 59.6% of successfully poisoned cases complete the full execute chain. — [arXiv 2607.27080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27080)
- **MPBench** [PRE, Jun 2026]. Average attack success 50.46%; average rate at which the agent retrieves and acts on the poisoned entry in a later unrelated session 41.05% (Hermes 64.70%, OpenClaw 17.40%). Search summary only: not verified. — [arXiv HTML 2606.04329](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04329v1)
- **PMPA** [PRE, Sep 2026]. Cross-session attack success 55.5% (OpenClaw) and 81.7% (Claude Code); a prompt-level defense gave limited protection once memory was already poisoned. Carried over. — [arXiv 2609.13889](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13889)
- **Agent goal self-propagation study** [PRE, Oct 2026; title not re-checked]. Written goals persisted through 100 unrelated sessions. Carried over. — [arXiv 2610.04083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04083)
- **Coding-trace staleness (MemTrace)** [PRE]. 57.0% of 9,226 file-bound traces saw a later change to a bound file; the estimated probability that bound files remain unchanged falls to about 0.50 after 1,000 event steps. — [arXiv HTML 2610.04838](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04838v1)

**Acting on superseded state**
- **"When Stale Constraints Go Unchecked"** [PRE, Aug 2026; single author; 16 models]. A consolidated memory states a constraint whose source was withdrawn; the provenance link is reachable. Agents made stale-consistent decisions 77.3% / 74.7% / 74.7% of the time (primary, replication, held-out domain) and inspected the critical provenance path in about 17% of episodes. Forcing one verification slot onto the critical path added 61 to 81 points. Carried over. — [arXiv 2608.25553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25553)
- **STALE** [PRE, May 2026]. 400 scenarios, 1,200 queries. Overall: Gemini-3.1-pro 55.2%, Qwen3.5-27B 31.3%, GPT-5.4 15.7%, GPT-4o-mini 8.7%. Memory frameworks: LightMem 17.8%, Mem0 8.3%, Zep 6.0%, A-MEM 5.1%; the authors' CUPMem prototype 68.0%. Recognition does not carry to use: Gemini-3.1-pro scores 92.0% on stating that a belief is outdated but 30.0% on rejecting a query that presupposes it. LightMem retrieved the updated evidence in 77.5% of cases yet still failed 56.1% / 99.0% / 78.6% of the three probes. Propagated ("Type II") invalidation, where an update to one attribute should invalidate a dependent belief without saying so, is harder: Gemini-3.1-pro falls from 92.0 / 30.0 / 71.0 to 69.0 / 14.0 / 55.0; Qwen3.5-27B from 76.0 / 4.0 / 39.0 to 42.0 / 3.5 / 23.0; Mem0 state resolution from 17.0 to 3.5. — [arXiv HTML 2605.06527](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06527v1)
- **StateMemBench** [PRE, Aug 2026]. Current-state accuracy at most 0.363 on 234 multi-session scenarios. Carried over. — [arXiv 2608.19652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19652)
- **Reflexion-style repetition** [workshop]. Reflection Repetition Rate 0.64 across trials despite environment resets. Carried over. — [arXiv 2605.29463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29463)

**What removes a wrong memory today**
- *Model rereads transcripts (dreaming).* One corrected note in the Mt. Moon case; Dreams replaces stale or contradicted entries using up to 100 past sessions. No correction rate, false-correction rate or latency published. [VENDOR] — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory); [Dreams docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/dreams)
- *User correction.* In 20,574 real coding-agent sessions from 1,639 repositories, 91.49% of visible misalignment resolutions required explicit user correction; 90.50% of episodes cost effort and trust rather than irreversible damage; patterns persist across adjacent sessions. [PRE, May 2026; abstract only] — [arXiv 2605.29442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29442)
- *Explicit forget request.* At most 1.5% of forget requests pass end to end; attack robustness at most 35.7. [PRE] — [arXiv HTML 2610.18052](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18052v1)
- *Same-key overwrite.* Solves 80.25% of MemoryAgentBench's conflict split, while multi-hop conflict resolution stays at about 7%. Carried over. — [critique listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/169013); [arXiv HTML 2507.05257](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05257v1)
- *Write-time filtering and history-based deletion.* A strict write evaluator plus history-based deletion raises EHRAgent to 42.06 and CIC-IoT to 89.60; with a coarse evaluator, history-based deletion lowers AgentDriver to 34.00 from 36.92. [ACL 2026 listing] — [arXiv HTML 2505.16067v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067v2)
- *Repair after diagnosis.* Removal passes in 86.3% of poisoned cases, with benign memory fully preserved in 62.5% and selective repair at 56.1%. [PRE] — [arXiv 2607.27080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27080)
- *Decay and eviction.* A budgeted curated-map memory scored 96% at 3 weeks but 72% at 9 weeks on evicted content, showing that eviction removes needed content rather than targeting wrong content. [PRE, Jul 2026] Carried over. — [arXiv 2607.21962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21962)
- *In-context only.* Thinking models do not self-condition on their own earlier errors and a sliding window helps on Markovian tasks; neither touches persistent memory. — [arXiv HTML 2509.09677v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09677v3)

### Inferences
- The default lifetime of a wrong note is indefinite. Every removal path found needs an external trigger (ground-truth transcript, user pushback, same-key newer statement, forced verification, or a diagnosed fault). None is triggered by the note's own dependency on something already known to be wrong.
- STALE Type II is the closest published measurement of the gap the design targets: when the invalidating update concerns a different item than the dependent, current models and memory systems lose 15 to 34 points of state resolution relative to same-key updates. An explicit dependency edge turns a Type II case into a lookup.
- The recognition-versus-use gap (92.0% vs 30.0%) and the stale-constraint result (provenance reachable, inspected 17% of the time) imply that marking a dependent as invalid is not enough; it should not be delivered. This matches the design's "withdraw so it stops influencing later work" wording, and it argues for measuring behaviour, not store contents.
- Dreaming is retrospective checking against the transcript. It can fix a note whose own facts contradict the record; it has no stated mechanism for withdrawing notes that were faithful to the transcript but learned under a route later found wrong.

### Gaps
- No published distribution of time-to-correction (sessions or days) for non-adversarial wrong notes in deployed agents. The Anthropic case is a single run with step counts only.
- No correction, miss or false-correction rates for dreaming or Letta-style sleep-time passes.
- No numbers found on how fast decay or "heat" scoring removes wrong, as opposed to merely old, memories.
- Several persistence numbers (PMPA, 2610.04083, stale constraints, StateMemBench) are carried over from earlier project notes and were not re-fetched; MPBench is from a search summary.

## 5. Evaluation designs that could measure route-level pruning

### Takeaway
No existing benchmark plants a wrong root decision with labelled dependents and independently supported items across sessions and then measures how long dependents keep being delivered. The components exist separately: paired clean/faulty traces with recovery, recurrence, benign-preservation and replay-cost metrics (rollback repair), trap/control suites with rollback recall and downstream harm (MemTX), write-execute-forget checkpoints (MemSecBench), propagated invalidation probes (STALE Type II), forget/control pairing with preservation and extraction-attack suites (Bemula), success by subtask depth across sessions (MemoryArena), and leave-one-out replay for causal influence (MemAudit).

### Cited Findings

**Closest existing designs and their metrics**
- **Rollback-repair controlled benchmark** [PRE]. 150 cases kept only if the injected fault changed the final answer and created downstream effects; 128 single-fault and 22 with two or three faults; paired clean and faulty traces, clean used only for scoring. Metrics: recovery, recurrence, faulty removal, benign preservation, claim-invalidation F1, replay ratio, LLM calls. It measures recovery after diagnosis and gives no estimate of natural fault rates or detector accuracy; faults are memories, not decisions; single session. — [arXiv HTML 2608.10502](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10502v1)
- **MemTX suites** [PRE]. 90-case main suite (60 traps, 30 controls) over six corruption families and a 56-case hardened suite of compound corruptions (40 traps, 16 controls). Metrics: task success, rollback recall, downstream harm on irreversible actions, legitimate-action allow rate (0.911 to 0.978 for all methods, so safety is not bought by refusing). Two invariants (action-safety gating, cascade-repair completeness) checked by property-based testing on 10,000 random traces and bounded enumeration of 5,530,160 states. — [arXiv HTML 2607.23929v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23929v2)
- **MemSecBench** [PRE]. Seven lifecycle checkpoints from write through persistence, recall, adoption and external consequence to removal and benign-memory preservation; selective repair requires both removal and preservation. — [arXiv 2607.27080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27080)
- **STALE** [PRE]. Type II scenarios are built by a "Logic Attacker" that finds an upstream attribute influencing a target and writes an update that never mentions the target; three probes separate knowing the state, resisting a query that presupposes the old state, and adapting downstream behaviour. — [arXiv HTML 2605.06527](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06527v1)
- **Bemula** [PRE]. Each forget dialogue has a control twin where the request is replaced by small talk, so a system that never stored the fact earns no credit; a preservation suite scores over-deletion; five extraction frames (indirect, role-play, jailbreak, Boolean, multi-hop) probe residual access; LLM judge agreed with a human on 98 of 100 items. — [arXiv HTML 2610.18052](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18052v1)
- **MemoryArena** [PR, ICML 2026]. Later subtasks strictly depend on earlier results (dependency chains up to depth four in travel; subqueries discarded unless they require earlier answers); reports success at subtask depth k. — [arXiv HTML 2602.16313](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16313v1)
- **StateMemBench** [PRE]. Answers are graded as current, superseded or other, which separates "delivered the stale dependent" from other errors. Carried over. — [arXiv 2608.19652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19652)
- **MemAudit** [PRE]. Counterfactual influence by removing one entry and replaying; a related paper notes single-entry removal misses interactions between entries and leaves multi-memory interventions to future work. — [arXiv HTML 2605.23723](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23723); [Visual Inception, arXiv 2604.16966](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.16966)
- **"Failure as a Process" timing metrics** [PRE]. Decisive-error step, lock-in step, recovery window and first-observable-signal step give a vocabulary for timing a planted root. — [arXiv HTML 2607.09510](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09510)
- **DreamBench-SWE** [PRE, Aug 2026; single author]. Multi-session software tasks where later tasks need evidence from earlier sessions, scored by hidden executable oracles; 180 scored items per condition; pass rates 21/180 without memory, 82/180 verbatim event memory, 97/180 one Mem0 configuration. The abstract does not say that wrong or superseded notes are planted. — [arXiv 2608.20664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20664)
- **SLUMP** [PR, Findings of EMNLP 2026]. 371 atomic verifiable components across 20 papers give per-commitment scoring of whether earlier design decisions are still honoured later. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.76/)
- **Ripple-effect metrics** [PR]. MQuAKE separates edit-wise success from multi-hop accuracy on questions whose answers should change; RippleEdits scores implied changes to related facts. — [arXiv HTML 2305.14795v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14795v3); [arXiv 2307.12976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12976)
- **Why a planted root is needed** [ICML 2025, not verified]. Post-hoc identification of the decisive step is 14.2% accurate, so natural failures do not give reliable root labels. — [arXiv 2505.00212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00212)

### Inferences
A design assembled from the pieces above (my synthesis, not a published benchmark):
- **Task construction.** Multi-session tasks with a planted wrong root decision R made in session 1 (a chosen approach that an executable oracle later shows to be wrong). Label every later memory as one of: D, dependent (true only if R holds, or produced by a step that consumed R); I, independent (learned while R was in force but supported by evidence that does not pass through R); U, unrelated control. Keep a case only if R changes downstream outcomes (rollback-repair filter). Add a Bemula-style control twin where R is correct, to price false retraction, and a STALE-style variant where the invalidating evidence never names the dependents.
- **Primary metric: sessions until no dependent is delivered.** After R is declared wrong at session t, count per session the D items delivered into context or acted on; report the first session with zero and the area under that curve. Baselines: no retraction; root-only deletion; same-key overwrite; dreaming-style transcript recheck; LLM-judge repair; full reset.
- **Residual behavioural influence.** Score behaviour, not store contents: probes that presuppose a retracted dependent (premise resistance), recurrence of the wrong approach in new tasks, and paired replays with and without the retracted set (leave-set-out, extending MemAudit's leave-one-out). The rollback-repair result (recurrence 0.266 after perfect faulty removal) shows this number will not be zero.
- **Over-pruning.** Benign preservation over I and U items, plus task success on later tasks that need an I item. Report it for in-force edges alone and for in-force edges plus an independent-support check, since that check is where the classical (DRed, counting) and 2026 (support check) literature locate the fix.
- **Edge-capture ablation.** Runtime-logged edges versus agent-declared edges versus post-hoc model-extracted edges, including a laundering case where knowledge is restated without declaring its parent (the case every backbone failed in MemTX).
- **Cost and detection kept separate.** Report replay ratio and LLM calls; and report results both with the root given (as rollback repair does) and with the root to be detected, because detection accuracy is low (14.2% step attribution; 28.8% best real-time recall) and would otherwise dominate.

### Gaps
- No benchmark found that matches route-level pruning end to end; the statement rests on a limited set of searches (MemoryArena, DreamBench-SWE, STALE, Bemula, MemSecBench, rollback repair and MemTX were the nearest hits).
- DreamBench-SWE's full task taxonomy (whether it includes stale or wrong notes) was not read; only the abstract.
- MemoryArena per-depth success values and RippleEdits per-criterion scores are in figures or full text I did not extract.
- No published metric for "sessions until no dependent is delivered" or any equivalent time-to-clean measure was found; the nearest are per-case recurrence (rollback repair) and per-checkpoint persistence (MemSecBench).
