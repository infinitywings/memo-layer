# Truth maintenance, belief revision and dependency-directed retraction, and their use in memory for LLM agents

Research date: 2026-11-13. "Classical" marks pre-LLM work (any year). LLM-era work is 2023 to November 2026; preprints and non-academic artifacts are flagged. Where a number was only seen in an abstract or a search snippet, that is stated.

## 1. Classical foundations: JTMS, reason maintenance, ATMS, dependency-directed backtracking, circular support, complexity

### Takeaway
A JTMS (Doyle 1979) keeps one IN/OUT labelling in which every believed node must have non-circular ("well-founded") support back to premises or assumptions; an ATMS (de Kleer 1986) instead stores, per node, the minimal sets of assumptions it rests on. With monotone (Horn) justifications labelling is unique and linear-time; out-list (non-monotonic) justifications make it NP-complete, order-sensitive and open to non-terminating odd loops, and ATMS labels are exponential in the worst case.

### Cited Findings

**Doyle's justification-based TMS (classical; Artificial Intelligence 12(3):231-272, 1979, peer-reviewed journal)**
- The TMS is a problem-solver subsystem that records and maintains the reasons for program beliefs; it grew out of Doyle's 1978 MIT master's thesis "Truth Maintenance Systems for Problem Solving". — [AITopics record of Doyle 1979](https://aitopics.org/doc/classics:CA80645E); [McAllester, AAAI-90](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1990/AAAI90-164.pdf)
- Data structures (as summarised by de Kleer): one *node* per problem-solver datum; *justifications* are inference steps from nodes to a node; the simplest, an *SL-justification*, has an *inlist* and an *outlist*. A node is *in* (believed) if it has a valid justification; a justification is valid if every inlist node is in and every outlist node is out. — [de Kleer 1986, sect. 3.1](https://www.dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf)
- Truth maintenance "finds an assignment of belief statuses (i.e., in or out) for every node such that every justification is satisfied", with the extra requirement that each node have well-founded support, "so circular supports which may satisfy the justifications locally are outlawed". One valid justification is selected as the node's current supporting justification. — [de Kleer 1986, sect. 3.1](https://www.dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf)
- An *assumption* is a node whose current supporting justification has a non-empty outlist (equivalently, whose well-founded support is non-monotonic). — [de Kleer 1986](https://www.dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf); [Doyle, IJCAI-79 "A Glimpse of Truth Maintenance"](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/1979/51)
- Incremental behaviour: when a new justification arrives the TMS checks whether it gives well-founded support to a currently unsupported node; on a change it updates the nodes whose well-founded arguments depend on the changed nodes. CP (conditional-proof) justifications are converted to an equivalent SL-justification by analysing the consequent's well-founded argument. — [Doyle 1979 full text (excerpts via search)](https://scispace.com/pdf/a-truth-maintenance-system-pim519w5js.pdf); [AITopics record](https://aitopics.org/doc/classics:CA80645E)
- IN means "believed", OUT means "not believed"; IN does not mean TRUE. P out and (not P) out is "don't know". — [Forbus, TMS lecture slides (Northwestern EECS 344)](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~forbus/c44/Lectures/TMS%20Intro.pdf)

**The simplified (monotone) JTMS as taught by Forbus (classical; university lecture slides, not peer-reviewed)**
- Justifications must be Horn clauses. Each node stores its *justifications* (where it is the consequent), its *consequences* (justifications that use it as antecedent), and a single *support* justification. Premises are always IN, assumptions are IN when enabled, contradiction nodes should never hold. A node is IN when it is an enabled assumption or premise, or some justification has all antecedents IN. "JTMS operations must preserve well-founded support." — [Forbus slides](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~forbus/c44/Lectures/TMS%20Intro.pdf)
- What can be retracted: premises, contradictions and justifications cannot; justifications "comprise the problem solver's cache". Only assumptions are retracted. Retraction algorithm: (1) make the assumption OUT, (2) retract all nodes that rely on it (propagate-outness), (3) find alternate support for the newly OUT nodes. The slides flag the failure of naive re-support through a cycle as "Loss of well-founded support!". — [Forbus slides](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~forbus/c44/Lectures/TMS%20Intro.pdf)
- A JTMS has no explicit representation of context: the context is implicit in the union of premises and enabled assumptions, which makes comparing two contexts hard and context switching potentially expensive. — [Forbus slides](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~forbus/c44/Lectures/TMS%20Intro.pdf)

**Dependency-directed backtracking (classical; Stallman and Sussman, Artificial Intelligence 9(2):135-196, 1977)**
- Citation and DOI 10.1016/0004-3702(77)90029-7. — [MaRDI portal record](https://portal.mardi4nfdi.de/wiki/Publication:1244202)
- Mechanism: on a failure, the justification records are used to find the subset of the current premise set that was used to derive the special proposition *contradiction*; a new constraint, a *nogood*, is installed stating that at least one of those premises must be false. — [McAllester, AAAI-90](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1990/AAAI90-164.pdf)
- In Doyle's TMS the contradiction is removed by picking one assumption (the culprit) and adding a justification for one of the nodes in its outlist. — [de Kleer 1986, sect. 3.1](https://www.dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf)

**McAllester's reason maintenance / clause-based TMS (classical; MIT AI Memo 473, 1978, technical report; survey "Truth Maintenance", AAAI-90 invited paper)**
- A monotonic TMS manipulates proposition symbols and Boolean constraints. The generic interface has `add-constraint` ("Once a constraint has been added it can never be removed") and `follows-from?(literal, premise-set)`. Justifications are required to be non-circular: if Q appears in the justification tree rooted at P, P must not appear in the tree rooted at Q. — [McAllester, AAAI-90](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1990/AAAI90-164.pdf)
- Boolean constraint propagation (BCP) derives labels by local propagation over single constraints, records a justification per derived label, and for clausal constraints "runs in linear time in the total size of the constraint set". BCP is logically incomplete (from P→Q and ¬P→Q it will not derive Q); full Boolean entailment is coNP-complete. — [McAllester, AAAI-90](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1990/AAAI90-164.pdf)
- Bibliographic record of the 1978 memo ("A three valued truth maintenance system", MIT AI Memo 473). — [Wikipedia, Reason maintenance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reason_maintenance)

**de Kleer's assumption-based TMS (classical; "An assumption-based TMS", Artificial Intelligence 28:127-162, 1986, peer-reviewed journal)**
- *Environment* = a set (conjunction) of assumptions. A node *holds* in environment E if it is derivable from E and the justifications J. An environment is inconsistent, a *nogood*, if false is derivable; every superset of a nogood is a nogood. A *context* is a consistent environment plus everything derivable from it. — [de Kleer 1986](https://www.dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf)
- A node's *label* is a set of environments that the ATMS keeps consistent (no environment is a nogood), sound (the node is derivable from each), complete (every consistent environment that derives the node is a superset of some label environment) and minimal (no label environment is a superset of another). Consequence: a node is derivable from E exactly when E is a superset of some label environment, so membership in a context is a subset test. An empty label means the node is not derivable from any consistent assumption set. — [de Kleer 1986](https://www.dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf)
- Label propagation: for a constraint (P ∧ Q) → R with C1 in P's label and C2 in Q's, C1 ∪ C2 is added to R's label; a consistency filter drops environments that contain a member of the label of *contradiction*, and a subsumption filter drops supersets. — [McAllester, AAAI-90](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1990/AAAI90-164.pdf)
- With n assumptions there are potentially 2^n contexts. — [de Kleer 1986, sect. 4.5](https://www.dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf)
- Circular justifications need no special check: with a⇒b and b⇒a alone both labels are empty, and any environment that travels round a loop is a superset of one already present, so minimality discards it. — [de Kleer 1986, sect. 5](https://www.dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf)
- Retracting a justification: "To make a justification retractable, it is conjoined with an extra assumption which represents its defeasability", and the solver retracts it by contradicting that assumption. Direct removal means invalidating the labels of all recursive consequents and recomputing; if false is among them, nogoods must be un-done and every label is potentially affected, so "directly removing a justifications is a bad idea". — [de Kleer 1986, sect. 4.9](https://www.dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf)
- de Kleer's list of JTMS limitations: the single-state problem (only one solution in view at a time); assumptions change identity as support changes; "Determining the well-founded support for a node requires a global constraint satisfaction process"; "Detecting loops of odd numbers of nonmonotonic justifications (which provoke infinite computation loops) is expensive"; unouting (deciding which previously derived, retracted data can be reasserted). — [de Kleer 1986, sect. 3.2](https://www.dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf)
- Scope caveat from de Kleer himself: conventional TMSs are oriented to finding one solution, the ATMS to finding all solutions; "The ATMS is not a panacea". — [de Kleer 1986](https://www.dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf)

**Complexity (classical)**
- BCP "is guaranteed to require at most linear space and linear time per query", whereas an ATMS "can require both exponential time and exponential space to answer a single query", and this worst case arises even with Horn clauses without implication cycles. The ATMS wins only where labels stay small and many contexts are queried. — [McAllester, AAAI-90](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1990/AAAI90-164.pdf)
- Provan (UBC Technical Report 88-11, 1988; technical report): deciding whether minimal supports exist for a Horn set is O(n), but generating the set of minimal supports is exponential in the number of assumptions for almost all Boolean expressions; constructing interpretations (maximal contexts) is NP-hard. By contrast, with a Horn database over n literals and O(n) clauses, "a JTMS can identify a solution in linear time, answer queries restricted to a single context in constant time with O(n²) space requirements, and update its database in O(n) time". — [Provan 1988](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/tr/1988/TR-88-11.pdf)
- Non-monotonic TMS: Elkan (Artificial Intelligence 43(2):219-234, 1990) characterises TMS labellings as stable models / autoepistemic expansions and proves that implementing a non-monotonic TMS is NP-complete; later work restates that incremental reasoning is NP-complete too. — [DBLP record](https://dblp1.uni-trier.de/rec/journals/ai/Elkan90.html); [TU Wien report RR-1702](https://haendel.kr.tuwien.ac.at/tr-reports/files/rr1702.pdf)
- Out-lists have two practical problems: "Beliefs become order-sensitive" and "Odd loops don't converge". — [Forbus slides](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~forbus/c44/Lectures/TMS%20Intro.pdf)
- Label explosion motivated follow-up designs: DeCoste and Collins (1991) report that standard ATMS labels grow combinatorially and propose compressed labels (CATMS); focused/best-first ATMS variants avoid generating all environments. — [DeCoste and Collins 1991](https://www.qrg.northwestern.edu/papers/Files/QRG_Dist_Files/QRG_1991/2_124_DeCoste_1991_CATMS_Label_Explosions.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Label" means two different things: in a JTMS it is the node's IN/OUT status; in an ATMS it is the set of minimal assumption sets under which the node holds. The design's "deterministic labelling over justifications" is a JTMS label; "which roots does this rest on" is an ATMS label.
- With Horn-only justifications the set of IN nodes is the least fixed point of forward propagation from premises and enabled assumptions, so the result is unique and independent of processing order; this is the property that makes the labelling a pure function of the log. Order-sensitivity and non-termination enter only through out-lists.
- JTMS retraction is over-delete then re-derive: everything downstream of the retracted assumption goes OUT, then alternate justifications are tried, and re-support must be checked against the nodes just marked OUT or a cycle can prop itself up (the "loss of well-founded support" case in Forbus's slides).

### Gaps
- Doyle's 1979 paper could not be machine-read in this pass (scanned PDFs; MIT DSpace returned HTTP 429). Its taxonomy of circular arguments (commonly described as three kinds: consistent with all nodes out, multiple admissible states, unsatisfiable) and the exact step list of the truth-maintenance process are therefore not quoted from the primary source. Wikipedia gives the page range as 251-272; other sources give 231-272, which is the better-supported range.
- Forbus and de Kleer, *Building Problem Solvers* (MIT Press, 1993) was not consulted directly; no sourced amortised bound for incremental JTMS relabelling beyond Provan's O(n) update statement.
- McAllester's 1978 and 1980 memos were not read; their content is taken from his AAAI-90 survey.

## 2. Belief revision and related formalisms, and which map onto an append-only graph with amendment nodes

### Takeaway
AGM theory is defined over logically closed belief sets and its Recovery postulate does not survive an immutable store; the variants that map cleanly are belief-base revision with kernel contraction (Hansson), grounded argumentation semantics (Dung), and why-provenance / PosBool provenance, each of which is a foundationalist reading of "a belief stands only while its recorded support stands". The Katsuno-Mendelzon split between revision (we were wrong) and update (the world changed) is the distinction an amendment node must carry.

### Cited Findings

**AGM (classical; Alchourrón, Gärdenfors and Makinson, Journal of Symbolic Logic 50:510-530, 1985)**
- Contraction postulates: Closure, Success, Inclusion, Vacuity, Extensionality, Recovery (plus Conjunctive overlap and Conjunctive inclusion). Revision postulates: Closure, Success, Inclusion, Vacuity, Consistency, Extensionality (plus Superexpansion and Subexpansion). Levi identity: K * p = (K ÷ ¬p) + p. — [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Logic of Belief Revision](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-belief-revision/)
- Recovery, K ⊆ (K ÷ p) + p, is "the most debated postulate of belief change", with standard counterexamples; it fails for belief bases. — [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-belief-revision/)
- Epistemic entrenchment (Gärdenfors 1988; Gärdenfors and Makinson 1988): postulates Transitivity, Dominance, Conjunctiveness, Minimality, Maximality; entrenchment-based contraction coincides exactly with transitively relational partial meet contraction. — [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-belief-revision/)
- Iterated revision: Darwiche and Pearl (Artificial Intelligence 89:1-29, 1997), postulates DP1-DP4. — [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-belief-revision/)

**Belief bases, kernel contraction, non-prioritized revision (classical)**
- Belief-base operations (Hansson 1991, 1999) act on a finite, not logically closed, set of sentences. Kernel contraction removes p by taking each *p-kernel* (a minimal subset that implies p) and applying an *incision function* that picks at least one element from each; every partial meet contraction on a base is a kernel contraction but not conversely. Core-retainment is the base-level minimal-change postulate. — [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-belief-revision/)
- The SEP cautions that "the distinction between operations on belief bases and operations on belief sets should not be equated with that between foundationalism and coherentism". — [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-belief-revision/)
- Non-prioritized change: semi-revision accepts an input only if it has more epistemic value than what it contradicts; screened revision (Makinson 1997) protects a core; credibility-limited revision (Hansson, Fermé, Cantwell and Falappa, JSL 66, 2001). — [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-belief-revision/)
- Revision versus update (Katsuno and Mendelzon 1992): revision incorporates new information about a fixed world; update records that the world has changed. — [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-belief-revision/)
- The SEP credits Doyle's 1979 TMS as important to the computer-science tradition of belief change but does not analyse reason maintenance. — [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-belief-revision/)
- A 65-page 2026 survey traces belief change from Doyle and London's 1980 taxonomy through AGM to current implementations, aiming at "computational blueprints" (peer-reviewed: European Journal on Artificial Intelligence, SAGE, 2026; only the abstract was read). — [Almeida and Casals, arXiv 2608.14567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14567)

**Abstract argumentation (classical; Dung, Artificial Intelligence 77(2):321-357, 1995)**
- The grounded extension is the least fixed point of the characteristic function (which maps a set of arguments to the arguments it defends) and is the least complete extension; in a finite framework it is reached by iterating from the empty set. — ["Extension-based Semantics of Abstract Dialectical Frameworks", arXiv 1405.0406 (restating Dung 1995)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.0406); ["More on extension-based semantics of argumentation", arXiv 2012.10592](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.10592)
- It can be computed in O(|V| + |E|) time. — [Nofal, Atkinson and Dunne, The Computer Journal 2019](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3062427/)
- A stable extension is conflict-free and attacks every outside argument; grounded and preferred extensions always exist, stable extensions may not, and odd-length cycles are a cause of non-existence. The well-founded model of a logic program corresponds to the grounded extension of the associated framework, and stable models to stable extensions. These statements come from search-result excerpts of later papers that restate Dung (1995) and Van Gelder et al. (1991); the excerpts were not matched to a single paper. — [arXiv 1907.09426](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.09426.pdf); [Wikipedia, Argumentation framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argumentation_framework)

**Provenance semirings and why-provenance (classical; Green, Karvounarakis and Tannen, PODS 2007, peer-reviewed)**
- Relations are annotated with elements of a commutative semiring K; union maps to + and join to ·. The polynomial semiring N[X] over tuple identifiers is the most general, and evaluation in any other semiring factors through it. — [Green et al., PODS 2007](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~green/papers/pods07.pdf)
- Why-provenance / lineage is the instance (P(X), ∪, ∪, ∅, ∅): the set of all contributing input tuples. The paper's own example shows its limit: two outputs with the same lineage {r, s} where one is derivable from s alone and the other from r alone, "and this cannot be detected by why-provenance" if one of r, s is less trusted. PosBool(B), positive Boolean expressions up to equivalence, keeps that distinction and is a finite distributive lattice. — [Green et al., PODS 2007](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~green/papers/pods07.pdf)
- Recursion: Datalog provenance can be infinite (infinitely many derivation trees), requiring ω-continuous semirings and formal power series; in N∞ the star 1* = ∞, while "in PosBool(B) we have e* = true for any e", so cycles are absorbed. — [Green et al., PODS 2007](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~green/papers/pods07.pdf)
- Complexity: for recursive (even linear-recursive) Datalog, deciding whether a set of facts is a member of the why-provenance is NP-complete in data complexity; it drops considerably for non-recursive queries; a follow-up shows minimal-why provenance is tractable in data complexity. — [Calautti, Livshits, Pieris and Schneider, arXiv 2303.12773 (PODS 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12773v1); [PACMMOD journal record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145%2F3651146)

**How LLM-era memory systems have instantiated these on append-only stores**
- Kumiho (arXiv preprint, March 2026): immutable revisions plus mutable tags; claims AGM K*2-K*6 plus Hansson's Relevance and Core-Retainment at belief-base level, explicitly rejects Recovery on the grounds of immutable versioning, leaves K*7/K*8 open. Typed edges include Depends_On, Derived_From, Supersedes. Contraction is not a cascade: dependents are only listed by a query-time `AnalyzeImpact` traversal as possibly needing re-evaluation. — [Park, arXiv 2603.17244](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17244v1)
- veritas-tms (software, alpha, self-described): combines JTMS-style propagation after Doyle with Hansson-style non-prioritized base revision, explicitly not classical AGM because weak newcomers are screened out; entrenchment is a fixed weighted score (0.40 source reliability, 0.25 log corroboration, 0.20 confidence, 0.15 recency with a 30-day half-life). — [veritas-tms 0.11.1 on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/veritas-tms/0.11.1/)
- BELIEFMEM (peer-reviewed, Findings of EMNLP 2026): AGM operators adapted to Hansson belief bases as the write semantics of a never-deleting fact base with predecessor pointers. — [Meng, Wu and McAuley, Findings of EMNLP 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.576/)

### Inferences
- Mapping table for an append-only typed graph with amendment nodes:
  - *AGM on belief sets*: poor fit. It needs logical closure, it is coherentist (no record of why a belief is held), and Recovery asks that retract-then-reassert restore the old state, which an immutable log neither needs nor provides. Kumiho's explicit rejection of Recovery is consistent with this.
  - *Belief-base revision with kernel contraction*: clean fit. A p-kernel is a minimal set of base items implying p, which is the same object as an ATMS label environment and a minimal why-provenance witness; the incision function is the culprit choice of dependency-directed backtracking. An amendment node is a recorded incision.
  - *Epistemic entrenchment*: fits only as the policy that chooses the culprit; it should be an explicit, logged input rather than a hidden score, since entrenchment orderings are not given by the log.
  - *Dung grounded semantics*: clean fit for amendments-as-attacks. It is unique, deterministic and linear-time, and it is sceptical: arguments on an unresolved odd cycle stay out. Stable/preferred semantics reintroduce multiple labellings and should be avoided for a deterministic validity function.
  - *Provenance semirings*: clean fit for the read side. PosBool/minimal-witness provenance answers "which root sets does this fact rest on" and equals the ATMS label; lineage (union of all contributing roots) is cheaper but cannot tell independent support from joint support, which is exactly the property the design needs for "nodes with independent support stay".
- The Katsuno-Mendelzon distinction should be a field on the amendment node. A *revision* amendment (the root was wrong) should withdraw dependents; an *update* amendment (the root was right then, the world changed) should close a validity interval without invalidating conclusions drawn while it held. P-Mem's separate `accept` (supersede) and `reject`/`revoke` operations (section 3) make the same split.
- If amendments may only target strictly earlier nodes, the attack relation is acyclic and all Dung semantics coincide on a single labelling. (Dung's result for well-founded frameworks is background knowledge here and was not re-verified from the primary source in this pass.)

### Gaps
- Dung 1995 and the complexity survey of Dvořák and Dunne (2017) were not read directly; statements about them come from later papers that restate them.
- The published link between ATMS and AGM (commonly attributed to Dixon and Foo, IJCAI 1993) and the delete-and-rederive view-maintenance algorithm (Gupta, Mumick and Subrahmanian, SIGMOD 1993) are relevant but were not sourced in this pass.
- "Circuits for Datalog Provenance" (Deutch, Milo, Roy and Tannen, ICDT 2014) was confirmed only as a citation; its claim about compact circuits for absorptive semirings is unverified here.
- No source was found that formalises prompt-context ("what was in the agent's context when this was learned") as a justification antecedent in any of these formalisms.

## 3. LLM-era work (2023 to November 2026) applying TMS, belief revision, argumentation or justification graphs to LLMs, memory, RAG or agentic KGs

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed work mostly puts a solver over LLM-generated beliefs for one question at a time (REFLEX, ArgLLMs) or applies AGM-style operators to conversational fact stores (BELIEFMEM); explicit TMS-style cascade retraction in agent memory exists only in 2026 preprints and open-source libraries, none with an independent head-to-head evaluation. In every system found, the model proposes the nodes and edges and code decides validity.

### Cited Findings

**Belief graphs with a constraint solver**
- REFLEX, "Language Models with Rationality" (Kassner, Tafjord, Sabharwal, Richardson, Schütze, Clark; EMNLP 2023, peer-reviewed). Mechanism: a frozen LLM (Entailer, T5-11B) builds a per-question belief graph by backward chaining, with statement nodes (true/false plus confidence) and rule nodes (p→h entailments, XOR between a statement and its negation, a multiple-choice rule); the graph is converted to weighted MaxSAT and solved with RC2 to find the cheapest set of belief flips, after which rules that conflict with the result are dropped. Model-checked: statements, rules, confidences. Code-checked: global consistency. — [arXiv 2305.14250](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14250)
- REFLEX numbers: consistency (1 − conditional constraint violation) rises 87.0→96.1 on EntailmentBank, 88.2→95.9 on OBQA, 85.7→96.6 on QuaRTz; answer accuracy is flat (79.4→79.9, 74.0→75.0, 80.2→80.0). Ablation on EntailmentBank dev: 96.1 full, 93.8 without p→h rules, 90.4 without XOR rules, 95.8 without the MC rule. Graphs are large (the main text says over 350 nodes and 80 constraints per question, the appendix says typically about 600 nodes); MaxSAT solving takes under 1 ms per question while each LLM call to expand or score a node takes about 2 s. Failure analysis: missing rules about 30%, incorrect beliefs about 30%, incorrect rules about 10%. — [arXiv 2305.14250](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14250)
- Predecessors named by REFLEX: BeliefBank (Kassner et al. 2021) used MaxSAT against given constraint rules; ConCoRD (Mitchell et al. 2022) used MaxSAT with NLI-derived constraints. (Both pre-2023, listed for lineage.) — [arXiv 2305.14250](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14250)

**Argumentation**
- ArgLLMs, "Argumentative Large Language Models for Explainable and Contestable Claim Verification" (Freedman, Dejl, Gorur, Yin, Rago, Toni; AAAI 2025 oral, peer-reviewed). Mechanism: the LLM generates supporting and attacking arguments (depth 1 or 2) and assigns each a base score, giving a quantitative bipolar argumentation framework; a gradual semantics (DF-QuAD) deterministically computes the claim's strength, thresholded at 0.5. Contestability is formalised: changing a base score or adding/removing an argument moves the claim strength in a provable direction. — [arXiv 2405.02079v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02079v3); [AAAI proceedings](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/33637)
- ArgLLM numbers (direct / chain-of-thought / ArgLLM with estimated base score, depth 1): GPT-4o mini 0.78/0.79/0.81 on TruthfulClaim, 0.77/0.75/0.74 on StrategyClaim, 0.74/0.72/0.71 on MedClaim; Mixtral-8x7B 0.77/0.76/0.81, 0.68/0.64/0.68, 0.60/0.61/0.61; GPT-4o on MedClaim 0.85/0.85/0.83. Accuracy is comparable to baselines, not better; the gain is a faithful, editable reasoning trail. — [arXiv 2405.02079v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02079v3)

**External edit memory**
- MeLLo (in the MQuAKE paper, Zhong, Wu, Manning, Potts, Chen; EMNLP 2023, peer-reviewed). Mechanism: edited facts live in an external store; the frozen LLM decomposes a multi-hop question, and at each hop a retrieved edit is checked (by the model) against its tentative answer. Multi-hop accuracy with k edits in memory on MQuAKE-CF: GPT-J 38.9 (k=1) → 14.2 (k=3000); GPT-3.5 52.1 → 30.9. On MQuAKE-T: GPT-3.5 91.1 → 85.5 (k=1868). Parameter editors at the same scale: MEMIT 11.8 → 5.4. — [arXiv 2305.14795](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14795)

**Belief-revision and provenance operators as memory write semantics**
- BELIEFMEM (Meng, Wu, McAuley; Findings of EMNLP 2026, peer-reviewed). An LLM-driven resolver classifies each extracted claim into one of four actions (labelled exp, rev, dup and con on the anthology page, which does not define the last two) within and across sessions over a similarity graph; nothing is deleted and superseded beliefs keep predecessor pointers; a per-query step chooses the head belief, the whole chain, or neither. Reported: 85.5% on LoCoMo (1,540 questions), 92.3% with a stronger backbone; 54.5% on BEAM (400 questions), 21.8 points above the LIGHT mean. The classification is model-checked; no propagation to derived beliefs is described on the anthology page. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.576/)
- Kumiho (Park; arXiv preprint, March 2026). Deterministic code implements revision, contraction, expansion and tag resolution on a Neo4j property graph; the agent's LLM fills structured fields and chooses the target item. Self-reported: LoCoMo token-F1 0.447 (n=1,540), adversarial refusal 97.5% (n=446); LoCoMo-Plus judge accuracy 93.3% (n=401; an independent reproduction is said to land in the mid-80% range; best cited baseline 45.7%); 49 AGM-compliance scenarios pass 100% (authors' own tests). No mention of TMS literature or circular support. — [arXiv 2603.17244](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17244v1)
- P-Mem, "Memory of Memory" (Qin and Lu, NUS; arXiv preprint, September 2026). Typed provenance graph with evidence, version, operation and tombstone nodes and supported-by / supersedes / alternative-of / revokes edges. A 4B LLM extracts keyed facts and emits a schema-constrained operation (accept, contest, reject, revoke, resolve, noop); code validates the operation (invalid ones fall back to noop), sets supersession direction from arrival order, and performs materialisation and rollback as a graph walk. Stated lineage is data provenance and temporal databases, not TMS or belief revision. — [arXiv 2609.25054](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25054v1)
- P-Mem numbers: MemoryAgentBench FactConsolidation at 6k/64k/262k, single-hop accuracy 95/95/88 with stale-answer rate 4.7/5.8/4.2, against Mem0 36/45/30 (stale 43.8/46.4/59.2), HippoRAG-v2 69/47/39 and Graphiti/Zep 15/32/12; multi-hop 41/21/11 (53/37/29 with iterative readout) against at most 17 for any baseline. Retraction probe (n=140): recovery of the displaced value is 100% with retained history, 0% for commit-without-retention, and 0% for Graphiti, which keeps the edge but has no reopen operation. Conflict probe: 97.9% post-resolution versus 57.1% raw store and 39.3% Graphiti. LongMemEval-s overall 52.6 versus MemoryOS 45.8 (+6.8, 95% CI [+2.4, +11.2]). Authors note about ±10-point intervals per cell and that contest/revoke were exercised mainly in controlled probes. — [arXiv 2609.25054](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25054v1)
- TEPA (Zhou, Ouyang, Zheng, Xiang; arXiv preprint, August 2026). Memory validity is an explicit state; a keyed precedent is revoked when fresh evidence contradicts it and revoked history is kept for audit. Abstract-level numbers: under full regime reversal (50 seeds) append-only 0.210, last-write-wins 0.210, no memory 0.309, TEPA 0.950. Whether revocation cascades to dependents is not stated in the abstract. — [arXiv 2608.07429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07429)
- "Reliable Post-Retrieval Assembly for Agent Memory" (Reddy and Challaram; arXiv preprint, poster at the COLM 2026 Lifelong Agent Workshop). Separates LLM evidence extraction from execution of the answer policy. On MemoryAgentBench FactConsolidation at 262K: 82%/93% single-hop and 27%/41% multi-hop with gpt-4o-mini/gpt-4o; controlled comparison +10.8 pp single-hop on average, of which only +2.0 pp comes from changing the final executor; no significant gain on LongMemEval (26/45 vs 29/45, p=0.45). — [arXiv 2606.01435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01435)

**Explicit TMS in agent memory (non-peer-reviewed)**
- veritas-tms (Python library, MIT licence, "3 - Alpha", v0.11.1 released 15 October 2026). JTMS-style propagation: beliefs carry justifications as OR over AND-sets of premise IDs; retracting a belief re-evaluates dependents and orphaned derived beliefs cascade out; well-foundedness is checked by requiring a directed path to a ground (directly observed) belief "so circular support does not survive"; an append-only audit log records assertions, retractions, cascades and rejections. The retraction decision is deterministic; contradiction *detection* is pluggable (heuristic, NLI model, or LLM-as-judge). Self-reported on 10,000 synthetic beliefs, single thread: about 58,000 bulk inserts/s, about 15,800 `assert_belief()` calls with contraction per second, 0.87 s for a full `find_contradictions()`. No accuracy evaluation on real data. — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/veritas-tms/0.11.1/)
- NeuSymMS (Sultan, Thuraisamy, Rajaratnam; arXiv preprint, May 2026, 7 pages). LLM extracts subject-relation-value triples; a CLIPS expert system classifies, deduplicates and reconciles them under explicit lifecycle rules. The abstract reports no experiments. — [arXiv 2605.17596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17596)
- Product and community artifacts seen only as search snippets: MnemeBrain (beliefs with supporting and attacking evidence, citing Doyle and de Kleer), Corollary (agent state as a dependency graph with IN/OUT nodes), and open GitHub design issues on alternative justification sets and an explicit "unsupported" state. — [MnemeBrain post](https://mnemebrain.ai/blog/mnemebrain-hn-post.html); [DEV Community post](https://dev.to/devactivity/beyond-context-rot-how-truth-maintenance-systems-drive-reliable-llm-agents-and-achieve-developer-3hkn); [symthaea issue 9048](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symthaea/issues/9048); [agent-memory issue 831](https://github.com/MythologIQ-Labs-LLC/agent-memory/issues/831)

**Guarding the evidence loop**
- "When Does Belief-Based Agent Memory Help?" (Singh; arXiv preprint, June 2026). Per entity-attribute categorical beliefs with closed-form Bayesian updating; on LoCoMo this gives little over last-write-wins because benchmarks rarely contain contradictory or differently reliable evidence; reliability estimated from text can be manipulated, so trust is capped by source provenance instead, which resists volumetric poisoning at a utility cost. Also reports a 27.5-point gap between token-F1 and LLM-judge scoring on identical outputs. — [arXiv 2606.22030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22030)
- "Closing the Feedback Loop" (Cui et al.; arXiv preprint, ICML 2026 RLxF workshop). Argues verbal-RL agents over-invest in experience extraction and under-invest in "insight governance"; proposes rules / evidence logs / skills layers with a non-monotonic rule lifecycle; states that the same accumulated experience either degrades performance below zero-shot or improves it depending on whether the curation loop is present (no numbers in the abstract). — [arXiv 2606.17591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17591)

### Inferences
- The division of labour is the same everywhere: the model produces nodes, edges, contradiction judgements or operation types; code computes the consequence. No system found has the model compute validity. The reliability ceiling is therefore the quality of model-produced edges (REFLEX's error analysis attributes about 70% of failures to missing rules, wrong beliefs or wrong rules rather than to the solver).
- Two families are distinct and often conflated. *Key-versioned supersession* (P-Mem, TEPA, BELIEFMEM, Kumiho, Graphiti) resolves conflicts between values of the same key; *justification-based retraction* (veritas-tms, the design under consideration) withdraws conclusions whose support is gone. Only the second handles "a root decision was wrong, withdraw what was built on it"; Kumiho stops at impact analysis and P-Mem's rollback walks a supersedes chain, not a dependency graph.
- REFLEX is coherentist (minimum-cost flips to restore consistency, any belief may flip) while a TMS is foundationalist (beliefs fall only when support falls). For a memory that must be auditable and replayable, the foundationalist reading is the one that yields a deterministic function of the log; MaxSAT over soft confidences can change many labels for one new input.
- No source found records the retrieved-memory set of an episode as a dependency of that episode's outcomes. The closest idea is provenance-capped trust (Singh), which bounds how much one source can move a belief, but does not exclude self-derived evidence.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper was found that implements Doyle-style or de Kleer-style dependency-directed retraction in LLM agent memory and evaluates it against a baseline. The only implementations are a library with synthetic micro-benchmarks and product posts.
- BELIEFMEM, TEPA, NeuSymMS, the Singh preprint and the Cui et al. preprint were read at abstract level only; mechanism details and result tables beyond the abstracts are unverified. The BELIEFMEM anthology abstract was partly garbled (baseline wording unclear).
- Mainstream temporal-KG memories (Zep/Graphiti, Mem0, HippoRAG) were not researched directly; their numbers here come from other papers' baselines.
- 2026 arXiv identifiers and venues are as returned by the pages fetched on 2026-11-13; later versions may differ.

## 4. Evidence that LLMs and agents fail at belief revision or at propagating a retraction

### Takeaway
The direct fact is updated; what follows from it is not. Parameter editing reaches about 90-100% on the edited fact and single digits to about 20% on multi-hop consequences; in-context revision shows a trade-off between updating and keeping beliefs; memory agents score at most 7% on multi-hop conflict resolution; and agents that store their own outputs propagate their own errors.

### Cited Findings

**Knowledge-editing ripple effects**
- MQuAKE (Zhong et al., EMNLP 2023, peer-reviewed): MQuAKE-CF has 9,218 instances (experiments on a 3,000 subset), MQuAKE-T 1,868. On MQuAKE-CF with GPT-J, edit-wise success versus multi-hop accuracy: ROME 88.3 vs 7.4 (19.9 with CoT), MEMIT 96.2 vs 7.0 (11.8), MEND 73.2 vs 8.0 (11.7), fine-tuning 46.9 vs 1.5; the unedited model answers 40.5% of the original multi-hop questions. On Vicuna-7B: ROME 99.7 vs 6.9 (12.1), MEMIT 95.8 vs 4.9 (6.7). On MQuAKE-T (GPT-J): ROME 100.0 vs 0.3 (11.3), MEMIT 100.0 vs 0.3 (4.8). — [arXiv 2305.14795](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14795)
- Scaling the number of edits makes it worse: MEMIT multi-hop (CoT) on MQuAKE-CF falls 11.8 → 5.4 from 1 to 3,000 edited instances, and on MQuAKE-T 4.8 → 0.0 at 1,868. (The paper's running text quotes MEND figures that do not match its Table 3; the table values are used here.) — [arXiv 2305.14795](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14795)
- RippleEdits (Cohen, Biran, Yoran, Globerson, Geva; published in TACL): about 5K factual edits (the abstract; section 4.2 lists 4,000) scored on Logical Generalization, Compositionality I and II, Subject Aliasing, Preservation and Relation Specificity. On the Popular subset, ROME on GPT-2 XL scores 5.7 on Logical Generalization and 21.8 on Compositionality II while scoring 100 on Subject Aliasing; on the Random subset ROME's Preservation is 9.9 on GPT-2 and 17.8 on LLaMA, i.e. unrelated facts about the subject are damaged. Editing methods average roughly 38-66% across criteria. — [arXiv 2307.12976v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.12976v2); [TACL listing](https://transacl.org/index.php/tacl/article/view/5739)
- Putting the edit in context instead of in weights is the strongest baseline: in-context editing averages 82.8 (LLaMA, Recent), 82.5 (LLaMA, Random) and 90.3 (GPT-3, Random), yet Logical Generalization stays at 33.3-71.1. — [arXiv 2307.12976v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.12976v2)
- Hase, Hofweber, Zhou, Stengel-Eskin and Bansal (TMLR 2024, peer-reviewed): frame model editing as belief revision, list 12 open problems (problem definition, benchmarks, whether LLMs have editable beliefs), and build a semi-synthetic Wikidata benchmark scored against an idealised Bayesian agent to measure the shortfall. — [arXiv 2406.19354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19354v1); [TMLR entry](https://mlanthology.org/tmlr/2024/hase2024tmlr-fundamental)

**In-context belief revision**
- Belief-R (Wilie, Cahyawijaya, Ishii, He, Fung; EMNLP 2024, peer-reviewed): about 2K items built on Byrne's suppression task with ATOMIC events (1,074 belief-update, 670 belief-maintain); about 30 models. Direct prompting: GPT-4 Turbo updates correctly in 16.76% of cases and maintains correctly in 86.72% (BREU 51.74); Llama-3 70B 10.99% / 92.09% (51.54); Mixtral 8x22B 35.38% / 36.57% (35.98). Models good at updating are poor at maintaining and vice versa; CoT and plan-and-solve prompting add about 1 BREU point on average. No human baseline is reported (inter-annotator Gwet's AC1 0.697 after filtering). — [arXiv 2406.19764](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19764); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.586.pdf)
- AGM-Bench (Jenkins; ICLR 2026 Workshop on Logical Reasoning of LLMs, workshop poster): 2,400 synthetic scenarios, seven frontier models, six AGM postulates plus Darwiche-Pearl. Models satisfy Success and Consistency at high rates but systematically violate Inclusion and Preservation; under iterated revision they show "belief inertia" (retaining retracted information) and "collateral damage" (retracting beliefs not logically affected); reasoning-trained models (o3-mini, DeepSeek-R1) are better at single-step revision but degrade faster under iteration. The page gives no rates. — [ICLR 2026 virtual site](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/10017503)
- Other evaluations seen as search snippets only: self-retraction depends on the model's momentary internal belief (COLM 2026); BeliefShift reports a trade-off between personalisation and resistance to drift across sessions (arXiv preprint, 2026). — [arXiv 2505.16170](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.16170); [arXiv 2603.23848](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23848v1)

**Memory agents**
- MemoryAgentBench (Hu, Wang, McAuley; arXiv preprint, July 2025): conflict resolution (FactConsolidation), single-hop / multi-hop accuracy: GPT-4o long-context 60.0 / 5.0; Claude 3.7 Sonnet 43.0 / 2.0; BM25 56.0 / 3.0; HippoRAG-v2 54.0 / 5.0; Mem0 18.0 / 2.0; MemGPT 28.0 / 3.0; Cognee 28.0 / 3.0; GraphRAG 14.0 / 2.0. The best multi-hop score in the table is 7.0 (Contriever), while the text says "at most 6%". With a 6K context, GPT-4o reaches 92.0 / 28.0 and o4-mini 100.0 / 80.0 (on a reduced set), collapsing to 88.0 / 10.0 and 61.0 / 14.0 at 32K. — [arXiv 2507.05257](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05257v1)
- In P-Mem's re-run of the same task with a 4B agent, CRUD-style Mem0 gives the stale value in 43.8-59.2% of single-hop answers and long-context in 79.7% at 6k. — [arXiv 2609.25054](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25054v1)

**Self-generated evidence and error propagation**
- Xiong et al., "How Memory Management Impacts LLM Agents" (arXiv preprint, May 2025, v2 October 2025): agents show "experience-following" (input similarity to a retrieved record predicts output similarity, Pearson r ≈ 1 for one agent); a retrieved record with errors is likely to be replicated and amplified, and if the resulting execution is added back to memory the error propagates to future tasks. Storing everything is worse than a frozen memory: add-all versus fixed memory versus strict selective addition is 13.05 / 16.75 / 38.50 on EHRAgent, 32.32 / 40.11 / 51.00 on AgentDriver, 59.90 / 71.50 / 85.40 on CIC-IoT, 55.48 / 67.53 / 70.95 on RegAgent. Some records with high input similarity consistently yield poor outputs ("misaligned experience replay"). — [arXiv 2505.16067v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067v2)

### Inferences
- The pattern across parameter editing, in-context revision and memory agents is the same: the direct fact is handled, its consequences are not. Asking the model to recompute what depends on a retracted item is the step that fails, which supports computing dependency closure in code over recorded edges.
- Belief-R's update/maintain trade-off and AGM-Bench's "collateral damage" are the model-side versions of over-retraction and under-retraction. A deterministic labelling removes both only to the extent that the recorded justifications are complete and not over-broad.
- Xiong et al. document the loop the design must rule out, but frame the fix as better admission and deletion policies; they do not record which memories were in context for an outcome, so they cannot discount self-derived evidence structurally.

### Gaps
- AGM-Bench violation rates are not on the workshop page; the paper itself was not located.
- No benchmark was found that tests multi-level retraction in agent memory directly (retract a root, then check that dependents at depth 2 or more are withdrawn while independently supported nodes survive). MemoryAgentBench multi-hop conflict resolution is the nearest proxy.
- No study was found that quantifies circular self-support in agent memory as a separate effect from general error accumulation.
- MemoryAgentBench's later publication venue was not verified.

## 5. Concrete guidance: which TMS variant fits an append-only log, cost at 10^4 to 10^6 nodes, pitfalls

### Takeaway
A monotone (Horn) JTMS recomputed as a least fixed point over the log is the fit: it is linear in nodes plus justification edges, unique, and matches the classical rule that justifications are never removed and only assumptions are switched. ATMS-style labels should be computed on demand for explanation and independent-support checks, not maintained globally, and amendments should enter as assumption defeat rather than as out-list justifications on ordinary nodes.

### Cited Findings
- Immutability is the classical default, not a deviation: in McAllester's interface a constraint once added "can never be removed"; in the Forbus JTMS justifications cannot be retracted because they are the solver's cache, and only assumptions are retracted. — [McAllester, AAAI-90](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1990/AAAI90-164.pdf); [Forbus slides](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~forbus/c44/Lectures/TMS%20Intro.pdf)
- The sanctioned way to withdraw an inference is to conjoin its justification with a defeasibility assumption and later contradict that assumption; removing the justification itself forces recursive label invalidation and possible nogood un-doing and is called "a bad idea". — [de Kleer 1986, sect. 4.9](https://www.dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf)
- Cost of the single-context option: propagation is linear in the total size of the (clausal) constraint set; a JTMS over a Horn database with n literals and O(n) clauses finds a solution in linear time, answers single-context queries in constant time, and updates in O(n). — [McAllester, AAAI-90](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1990/AAAI90-164.pdf); [Provan 1988](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/tr/1988/TR-88-11.pdf)
- Cost of the all-contexts option: exponential time and space in the worst case even for acyclic Horn clauses; 2^n potential contexts for n assumptions; label generation exponential for almost all inputs; Provan concludes "it will be infeasible to solve moderately large problems without problem restructuring". de Kleer notes bit-vector environments cost time proportional to the number of assumptions ever created. — [McAllester, AAAI-90](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1990/AAAI90-164.pdf); [Provan 1988](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/tr/1988/TR-88-11.pdf); [de Kleer 1986](https://www.dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf)
- Grounded argumentation labelling is O(|V| + |E|). — [Nofal, Atkinson and Dunne 2019](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3062427/)
- Empirical data point (self-reported, synthetic, Python, single thread): at 10^4 beliefs a JTMS-style store does about 15,800 assert-with-contraction operations per second; a full contradiction scan takes 0.87 s. — [veritas-tms on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/veritas-tms/0.11.1/)
- For comparison, the model-side cost dominates: in REFLEX the solver takes under 1 ms per roughly 600-node graph while each LLM call to create or score a node takes about 2 s. — [arXiv 2305.14250](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14250)
- Circular support: JTMS semantics outlaw supports that satisfy justifications only locally; ATMS label minimality makes a loop contribute nothing; PosBool provenance absorbs cycles (e* = true); veritas-tms implements this as "must have a directed path to a ground belief". — [de Kleer 1986](https://www.dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf); [Green et al., PODS 2007](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~green/papers/pods07.pdf); [veritas-tms on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/veritas-tms/0.11.1/)
- Non-monotonic justifications: NP-complete to label, also incrementally; order-sensitive; odd loops do not converge and are expensive to detect. — [DBLP record of Elkan 1990](https://dblp1.uni-trier.de/rec/journals/ai/Elkan90.html); [TU Wien report RR-1702](https://haendel.kr.tuwien.ac.at/tr-reports/files/rr1702.pdf); [Forbus slides](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~forbus/c44/Lectures/TMS%20Intro.pdf); [de Kleer 1986](https://www.dekleer.org/Publications/An%20Assumption-Based%20TMS.pdf)
- Exact "which root sets" queries are hard under recursion: why-provenance membership for recursive Datalog is NP-complete in data complexity, while minimal-why provenance is tractable. — [Calautti et al., arXiv 2303.12773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12773v1)
- Lineage alone cannot separate joint from independent support. — [Green et al., PODS 2007](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~green/papers/pods07.pdf)
- Observed pitfalls in LLM-era systems: write-time materialisation loses to read-time pruning on natural conversation because extraction misses passing mentions and keys fragment (P-Mem); a temporal KG that retains invalidated edges but has no reopen operation recovers 0% of displaced values after a retraction (Graphiti, as measured by P-Mem); contradiction detection remains model- or heuristic-judged even when retraction is deterministic (veritas-tms). — [arXiv 2609.25054](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25054v1); [veritas-tms on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/veritas-tms/0.11.1/)

### Inferences
**Recommended variant.** Monotone JTMS, recomputed (or incrementally maintained) as the least fixed point over the append-only log:
- Node kinds map to classical roles: observations are premises; decisions and claims adopted without derivation are assumptions; derived claims and procedures are consequents of Horn justifications, each justification being an AND-set of antecedent node IDs, with several justifications per node meaning OR.
- An amendment node disables an assumption or records a nogood over a set of assumptions. Per de Kleer's device, every justification can carry its own defeasibility assumption, so an amendment can withdraw one inference step without touching the antecedents. Nothing is deleted; the label changes.
- Validity = IN under the least fixed point. This gives exactly the stated requirement: dependents of a defeated root go OUT, and a node with another justification whose antecedents are still IN stays IN, provided re-support is computed from premises upward rather than by local repair.
- Keep amendments stratified: an amendment may target only earlier nodes, and its own validity may be attacked only by a later amendment. The attack relation is then acyclic, so there are no odd loops and the labelling stays unique and polynomial even though amendments are logically non-monotonic. Do not allow out-lists on ordinary derived nodes ("believe X unless Y is believed"); that is the door to Elkan's NP-completeness and to order-dependence.

**Circular support.** The classical machinery prevents a node from supporting itself only through recorded edges. To rule out "memory M was delivered, behaviour followed, the outcome is counted as evidence for M", the outcome's justification has to list M (the delivered context) among its antecedents. Then any support for M that runs through that outcome is a cycle and gives no well-founded support; in ATMS terms the candidate environment contains M's own assumption and is not an independent environment. A simple operational rule that follows: evidence e counts toward M only if M's assumption does not appear in e's provenance. Two cautions: (i) this is a causal-influence dependency, not a logical derivation, so the outcome observation itself should remain IN as an observation, and only its role as support for M should be discounted; (ii) recording the whole prompt as antecedents makes everything depend on everything and causes over-retraction, so the context dependency should be a separate edge type used for the independence test, not for cascade invalidation.

**Cost estimates (derived from the linear bounds above, not measured).** A full relabel visits each node and each justification edge once. At 10^4 nodes this is negligible; at 10^6 nodes with a few antecedents per justification it is on the order of 10^6 to 10^7 edge visits, which is sub-second in compiled code and seconds in interpreted code, so full recomputation from the log on every amendment is viable and keeps the function replayable. Incremental maintenance (over-delete downstream, then re-derive) costs time proportional to the affected subgraph and is only worth adding if amendments to high-fan-out roots are frequent. ATMS labels are the risk: a node with k antecedents each having m alternative supports can have up to m^k environments, so maintain no global labels; compute minimal supporting root sets on demand for one node with a cap on count and size, and fall back to lineage (the union of contributing roots, linear) when the cap is hit, while remembering that lineage cannot show independence.

**Pitfalls checklist.**
1. Label explosion if ATMS labels are maintained eagerly (classical; exponential).
2. Out-list justifications on derived nodes: NP-complete labelling, order-sensitivity, odd loops.
3. Re-support through a cycle after retraction (loss of well-founded support); always re-derive from premises.
4. Treating update as revision: a superseded-because-the-world-changed root should close a validity interval, not invalidate conclusions drawn while it held.
5. Entrenchment hidden in a score: if the system auto-selects which root to defeat on contradiction, the choice must be logged as an amendment with its reason, or replay is no longer deterministic with respect to the log alone.
6. Edge quality is the ceiling: the labelling is exact only relative to justifications the model extracted; missing edges cause under-retraction (stale dependents survive), over-broad edges cause collateral damage. Evaluate edge recall and precision separately from the labelling.
7. Contradiction detection is a separate, model-judged step in every system surveyed; keep it outside the validity function and make its outputs ordinary nodes with their own justifications.
8. Key fragmentation in write-time extraction (P-Mem's reported limitation) applies to justification targets too: an amendment aimed at the wrong duplicate leaves the real root IN.
9. Retain defeated nodes with enough structure to reopen them; a store that keeps history without a reopen path recovers nothing after a retraction of a retraction.

### Gaps
- No published measurement of JTMS or ATMS relabelling at 10^5 to 10^6 nodes was found; the only timing data is one library's synthetic micro-benchmark at 10^4. The cost estimates above are derived from asymptotic bounds.
- No source evaluates the proposed self-provenance exclusion rule for evidence; it is an inference from well-founded support, ATMS label minimality and PosBool absorption, and its effect on learning speed (how much valid evidence it discards) is unknown.
- No source gives typical label sizes for agent-memory dependency graphs, so the practical severity of label explosion in this setting is unmeasured.
- The interaction of validity intervals (bitemporal supersession) with dependency-directed retraction is not treated in any source read; TOKI (arXiv 2606.06240, a bitemporal operator algebra for agent memory) surfaced in search but was not read.
