# Agent memory: scope, applicability and staleness; self-written lessons vs user-stated rules

Compiled 2026-11-13. Scope: mid-2025 to Nov 2026, with earlier foundations marked **[earlier]**. Status labels: **[peer-reviewed]** = main-track or demo paper at a named venue; **[workshop]**; **[arXiv-only]** = preprint with no venue listed; **[industry post]** = company engineering or research blog, not peer-reviewed. Items marked "(not re-fetched)" come from my prior knowledge of the paper and were not re-checked against the source in this session; treat their exact numbers with care.

---

## (A) How systems decide a stored memory applies now, and when it stops applying

### Takeaway
Almost every system handles validity at the level of a single fact or key. New evidence on the same key updates, invalidates or revokes the old record (Mem0, Zep/Graphiti, Memory-R1, TEPA), and memories age out through generic decay, "heat" or budget scores. Very few attach explicit applicability conditions to a memory; AutoGuide (2024) is the clearest example. The 2026 benchmarks agree that storing the update is not the hard part. Agents keep acting on the superseded state: the best model on STALE reaches 55.2% overall, current-state accuracy on StateMemBench stays at or below 0.36, and bounded self-maintained memory drops LongMemEval knowledge-update accuracy from 92% to 77% on a frontier model. A last-write-wins rule per key solves most single-hop updates. Implicit, multi-hop and dependent-state invalidation are unsolved.

### Cited Findings

**A1. Fact-level supersession and invalidation mechanisms**
- **Zep / Graphiti** [arXiv-only; Jan 2025; Zep AI]. Mechanism: a bi-temporal knowledge graph. Timeline T (valid time) gives each edge `t_valid` and `t_invalid`. Timeline T' (transaction time) gives `t'_created` and `t'_expired`. When a new edge arrives, the system retrieves semantically related edges and an LLM checks them for contradiction. If a contradiction overlaps in time, the old edge's `t_invalid` is set to the new edge's `t_valid`. The old edge is kept, not deleted, and newer information wins in T' order. Numbers: on LongMemEval_S (~115k tokens), gpt-4o-mini goes from 55.4% (full context) to 63.8% (Zep), and gpt-4o from 60.2% to 71.2% (+18.5% relative), with latency down about 90% (28.9 s to 2.58 s for gpt-4o) using about 1.6k context tokens. Knowledge-update questions fell with gpt-4o-mini (76.9% to 74.4%) and rose with gpt-4o (78.2% to 83.3%). Temporal reasoning rose 36.5% to 54.1% (mini) and 45.1% to 62.4% (4o). Single-session-assistant accuracy declined for both models. The authors say weaker models may struggle to use the temporal data. On DMR the gain is marginal (94.8% vs MemGPT 93.4%). — [Zep arXiv 2501.13956](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13956)
- **Mem0 / Mem0g** [arXiv-only, Apr 2025] (not re-fetched). Mechanism: an LLM extracts candidate facts, compares each with similar stored memories, and picks ADD, UPDATE, DELETE or NOOP through a tool call. The graph variant marks conflicting relations as invalid instead of deleting them, to keep temporal reasoning possible. Numbers from the abstract: +26% relative LLM-as-judge over OpenAI memory on LoCoMo, 91% lower p95 latency, and over 90% token savings versus full context. — [Mem0 arXiv 2504.19413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19413)
- **Memory-R1** [peer-reviewed, ACL 2026 long; arXiv Aug 2025]. Mechanism: a Memory Manager trained with RL (PPO or GRPO) chooses ADD, UPDATE, DELETE or NOOP for each new fact, and a separate Answer Agent filters retrieved memories. In the motivating example, a vanilla manager treats a second dog adoption as a contradiction and issues DELETE+ADD, while the trained manager issues one UPDATE that merges both facts. Numbers: with LLaMA-3.1-8B on LoCoMo, it beats Mem0 by +48% F1, +69% BLEU-1 and +37% LLM-judge (relative). It used only 152 training QA pairs and generalizes to MSC, LongMemEval and 3B–14B models. — [Memory-R1 search summary / ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.583.pdf); [arXiv 2508.19828](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19828v5)
- **Mem-α** [arXiv-only; Sep 2025]. Mechanism: RL learns memory construction over incoming chunks into three parts: core (running summary), semantic (factual statements) and episodic (time-stamped events). The reward combines QA accuracy, compression and memory-content correctness. Numbers: trained on sequences of at most 30k tokens, it generalizes to more than 400k (over 13×). It has no explicit validity or scope fields. — [Mem-α arXiv 2509.25911](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25911v1); [HF page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.25911)
- **A-Mem** [peer-reviewed, NeurIPS 2025]. Mechanism: Zettelkasten-style notes with keywords, tags and a context description, plus links between notes. "Memory evolution" rewrites the context and attributes of existing notes when a related new note arrives. Old content is reinterpreted rather than invalidated. The abstract reports gains over SOTA on six foundation models without headline numbers. — [NeurIPS 2025 proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/19909c36f51abc4856b4560aff3d36d6-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **MemoryOS** [arXiv lists no venue; May 2025. I recall EMNLP 2025 but did not re-verify]. Mechanism: short-, mid- and long-term (persona) tiers. Short to mid uses a dialogue-chain FIFO, and mid to long uses segmented pages. The paper body also describes a "heat" score (visit count, interaction length, recency decay) that promotes hot segments to long-term persona and evicts cold ones (not re-fetched). Numbers: +49.11% F1 and +46.18% BLEU-1 on LoCoMo with GPT-4o-mini. — [MemoryOS arXiv 2506.06326](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06326)
- **MemOS** [arXiv-only; Jul 2025]. Mechanism: the MemCube unit bundles content with metadata that includes provenance and versioning. The project docs describe a lifecycle of Generated → Activated → Merged → Archived → Frozen with version rollback. Its benchmarks concern LoCoMo-style QA; nothing independent tests the governance features. — [MemOS arXiv 2507.03724](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.03724); [MemOS docs](https://memos-docs.openmem.net/home/memos_intro)
- **LightMem** [peer-reviewed, ICLR 2026]. Mechanism: a "sleep-time update" first stores new entries with timestamps as soft updates, then reorganizes and de-duplicates them offline, so maintenance happens outside inference. Numbers vary by version. v4 reports LongMemEval accuracy +2.09–6.40% (GPT) and up to +7.67% (Qwen), with online-only token cost down up to 105.9×/117.1× and API calls down up to 159.4×/309.9×. The v1 abstract said up to +10.9% accuracy. — [LightMem ICLR 2026 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10008366); [arXiv 2510.18866v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18866v4)
- **HippoRAG 2** [peer-reviewed, ICML 2025]. Mechanism: Personalized PageRank over a KG that also contains passage nodes, framed as non-parametric continual learning. Numbers: +7% on associative-memory tasks over the best embedding model. The abstract describes no way to invalidate or retire outdated facts. — [HippoRAG 2 arXiv 2502.14802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14802)
- **TEPA** [arXiv-only; Aug 2026]. Mechanism: memory validity is an explicit state. Observations are stored as keyed precedents tied to the evidence that created them. Fresh evidence that contradicts an active precedent under the same key revokes it, and the revoked record is kept for audit and can be re-promoted later. Numbers: under controlled drift with full reversal (50 seeds), TEPA scores 0.950, append-only and last-write-wins 0.210 each, and no memory 0.309. Under real file-backed execution drift: TEPA 0.950, append-only 0.203, no memory 0.298. On MemoryAgentBench SH-6k it only matches last-write-wins (0.890 vs 0.630 without revocation). The authors flag multi-hop and very long context as limits. — [TEPA arXiv 2608.07429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07429)
- **StateMem / StateMemBench** [arXiv-only; Aug 2026; Han group, UIUC]. Mechanism: "state-first" memory that models supersession and dependencies between facts explicitly, also offered as a single-call wrapper. Numbers on 234 multi-session scenarios: current-state accuracy rises 0.205 to 0.363 over the strongest same-backbone baseline (DeepSeek-V4-Flash) and 0.149 to 0.233 over the strongest memory system (Qwen-3.5-9B). Wrapping six backends adds 32–67 points, of which 15–32 points survive a length- and cost-matched control. Answers are graded as current, superseded or other. — [StateMemBench arXiv 2608.19652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19652)
- **Hindsight** [peer-reviewed system demo, ACL 2026; arXiv Dec 2025]. Mechanism: four networks (world facts, agent experiences, observations, opinions). Opinions carry confidence scores that are updated as evidence arrives. Numbers: LongMemEval rises from 39.0% (full-context baseline) to 83.6% with a 20B open model, and reaches 91.4% with Gemini-3 Pro. These are self-reported. — [Hindsight arXiv 2512.12818](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2512.12818v1); [ACL author listing](https://aclanthology.org/people/christopher-latimer/)

**A2. Benchmarks and studies of outdated memory (measured effects)**
- **LongMemEval** [earlier; peer-reviewed, ICLR 2025] (not re-fetched). Five abilities, including knowledge updates, temporal reasoning and abstention. Commercial assistants and long-context LLMs lose about 30% accuracy over sustained interactions. — [arXiv 2410.10813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813)
- **MemoryAgentBench** [peer-reviewed, ICLR 2026; Hu, Wang, McAuley]. Four competencies, including "conflict resolution / selective forgetting" built on FactConsolidation. All methods (long-context and RAG) fail at multi-hop updating, dropping as low as about 7%. Long-context agents do reasonably on single-hop. — [arXiv 2507.05257v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05257v3)
- **Critique of MemoryAgentBench conflict split** [NeurIPS 2026 listing; I did not retrieve the title or verify the venue type]. A frozen "newest statement about a fact wins" rule answers 80.25% of questions (74.5% on held-out lists). Long-context models score about 83–85% on items that rule solves and about 10–12% on items whose gold answer the last-write graph cannot reach. This suggests the split partly measures label reachability, not forgetting. — [NeurIPS 2026 virtual page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/169013)
- **STALE** [arXiv-only; May 2026]. Measures "implicit conflict", where a later observation invalidates a memory without explicitly negating it. 400 expert-validated scenarios and 1,200 queries over 100+ topics, with context up to 150k tokens. Three probes: state resolution (is the belief outdated?), premise resistance (reject queries that assume the stale state) and implicit policy adaptation (apply the new state downstream). The best model scores 55.2% overall. Models accept stale premises embedded in queries and miss that a change in one aspect of the user's state should invalidate related memories. Its baseline, CUPMem, adds write-time state consolidation and propagation-aware search. — [STALE arXiv 2605.06527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06527)
- **StateAuditor** [arXiv-only; Aug 2026]. Diagnosis: checking the draft response misses stale dependencies, because open-ended answers rarely state the stale value outright. Mechanism: audit from stored state toward the draft. An LLM proposes old-to-new transitions from timestamped evidence. Deterministic code then ties each quote to one entry and checks that the new evidence is actually later, and only verified transitions trigger repair. It checks provenance and chronology, not semantic supersession. Numbers on STALE (400 scenarios, 50-session histories): 0.736 vs 0.686, a paired +5.0 points (95% CI +2.9 to +7.2). An independent judge gives 0.738 vs 0.680. A matched control with the same evidence and call budget gives 0.692 (+0.6, not significant). On HorizonBench, most of the gain came from the draft-side audit, and a harder lifecycle set showed no gain. — [arXiv 2608.01619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01619)
- **Supersede** [arXiv-only; Jun 2026; single author]. On the LongMemEval knowledge-update subset, gpt-5.4 drops from about 92% (full context) to 77% (bounded self-maintained memory), McNemar p<0.005. Growing the conversation 24× drops accuracy from 68% to 28%, and extra memory gives 28% to 28% (n=25), so failure scales with length, not compression. GRPO on Qwen2.5-3B in the released RL environment improves held-out supersession from 9.0% to 16.7% in a single run. — [Supersede arXiv 2606.27472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27472)
- **Memory Trust Gap** [workshop submission, under review at a NeurIPS 2026 workshop; Sep 2026]. Benefit suite (the stored fact is needed): models use the stored value 0.92–1.00 of the time. Safety suite (a reliable tool holds the correct value): harm grows with capability, and the largest Qwen3 (8B) fails most when a stale note looks current. In a 2×2×2×2 factorial design, removing the memory label increases over-trust at every size, a recency cue fools larger models more, and source authority has little effect. Exposing metadata helps capable models, while smaller ones need the conflict resolved for them beforehand. The pattern replicates on Llama-Instruct, RGB and MisBench. — [arXiv 2609.01852](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01852)
- **Stale constraints in inherited memory** [arXiv-only; Aug 2026; single author; 16 models]. Setup: a consolidated memory states a constraint whose source record has since been withdrawn, the provenance link is still reachable, and the agent may inspect only 2 of 6 records. Agents inspected the constraint's provenance in about 1 of 5 episodes and made stale-consistent decisions 77.3% / 74.7% / 74.7% of the time (primary / replication / held-out domain). Forcing one budget slot onto the critical path recovered +61 to +81 points, though this uses experimenter knowledge. A one-sentence, target-blind rule ("favor memories that state a limit on a candidate action") gave +89.3 points. A content-free freshness cue changed nothing. Path selection was 17.0% at 2 slots and 88.7% at 4 of 6. — [arXiv 2608.25553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25553)
- **Veracium / "Ground Truth First"** [arXiv-only; Jul 2026; single author]. A longitudinal benchmark with gold answers built before the conversations. It has per-fact validity intervals, volatility classes, source channels, sent-vs-received trust and as-of-date questions (about 380 questions, 15 types). Rankings invert with tenure: a budgeted curated-map memory scored 96% at 3 weeks but 72% at 9 weeks on evicted content, while a provenance-typed graph rose to 90% (inversion held for 6/6 users, p=0.031). Full-history rendering matched the best system early, with no clear edge later, at about 2× read cost. Weakly written facts failed 24% of the time vs 2% for well-written ones. — [arXiv 2607.21962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21962)

**A3. Decay, forgetting and retirement policies**
- **MemoryBank** [earlier; peer-reviewed, AAAI 2024] (not re-fetched). Ebbinghaus-curve decay, where retrieval reinforces a memory's strength. — [arXiv 2305.10250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10250)
- **FadeMem** [arXiv-only; Jan 2026]: exponential Ebbinghaus-style decay whose rate depends on importance, with two memory layers and LLM-guided conflict resolution. **Oblivion** [arXiv-only; Mar 2026]: forgetting means reduced accessibility rather than deletion, with separate read and write control paths. **Adaptive budgeted forgetting** [arXiv-only; Apr 2026]: keeps memories under a storage budget by recency, frequency and semantic similarity, reporting LoCoMo F1 above a 0.583 baseline and fewer false memories. I saw only search snippets for all three, not full results. — [FadeMem 2601.18642](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.18642); [Oblivion 2604.00131](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00131v1); [budgeted forgetting 2604.02280](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02280v1)
- **Xiong et al., "How Memory Management Impacts LLM Agents"** [peer-reviewed, ACL 2026; arXiv May 2025]. Combining *selective addition* (quality-check before storing, ideally with an evaluator) and *deletion* (drop records by recent retrieval frequency, and drop low-quality ones) gives about +10% absolute over naive memory growth. I took this figure from summaries, not the full tables. — [arXiv 2505.16067v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067v2); [ACL Anthology preview](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.acl-long.27/)
- **ReasoningBank** [peer-reviewed, ICLR 2026] ablation: retrieving too many experiences lowers success rate. — [arXiv 2509.25140](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.25140)
- **Anthropic, "The components of agent memory"** [industry post; 6 Nov 2026]. Memory drifts as environments change and checking every memory may be impractical, so the post suggests focusing verification on volatile information or tuning "dreaming" (offline consolidation) cadence by access frequency. Preloaded memory should stay small because a wrong note affects every session; Claude Code loads only the first 200 lines or 25 KB of `MEMORY.md`. Dreaming can edit, merge and prune, but the post gives **no explicit retirement policy**. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)
- Caution on usage-based retention: a self-graded episodic memory inflates rewards for confident mistakes, so they are preferentially reused (the "Echo Gap"; see B3). Retrieval alone also creates a popularity loop independent of evidence. That second point is argued in practitioner writing, not shown experimentally. — [Memory Reward Inflation arXiv 2608.00017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00017); [practitioner blog (not peer-reviewed)](https://www.glukhov.org/ai-systems/memory/self-reinforcing-memory-loops)

**A4. Applicability conditions and context-dependent retrieval**
- **AutoGuide** [earlier; peer-reviewed, NeurIPS 2024]. The closest prior art to "deliver only when scope covers the state". Guidelines extracted from offline experience have a conditional form ("in context X, do Y"). A context-identification module labels the current state, and only guidelines whose generated context matches are injected. This contrasts with ExpeL, which puts all insights in the prompt. An ablation shows that determining each guideline's applicability from its context matters. — [NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/d8efbb5dd415974eb095c3f06bff1f48-Abstract.html); [arXiv 2403.08978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08978)
- **ExpeL** [earlier; peer-reviewed, AAAI 2024] (not re-fetched). Insights are edited with ADD, EDIT, UPVOTE and DOWNVOTE. Each carries an importance count and is removed when the count reaches zero. This is the earliest instance of "demote on counter-evidence, retire at zero". — [arXiv 2308.10144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10144)
- **Misaligned experience replay** (Xiong et al., ACL 2026). Some records look similar to the current task but consistently produce dissimilar or poor outputs when retrieved. Surface similarity is a poor proxy for applicability. — [arXiv 2505.16067](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067v2)
- **CUPID** [arXiv Aug 2025; I recall COLM 2025 but did not re-verify]. 756 human-curated session histories with context-dependent preferences. Ten LLMs fail to tell which earlier context applies to a new request, scoring under 50% precision and under 65% recall when inferring preferences. — [CUPID arXiv 2508.01674](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2508.01674)
- **Execution provenance for retrieval** [arXiv-only; Sep 2026]. Retrieval units built from tool arguments and outputs beat flat 512-token windows by +19.07 points (Full Support@2048), and still +11.96 over the best of four flat chunk sizes. A typed-provenance R-GCN adds +4.55 (95% CI 2.98–6.18), mainly when evidence spans several events. — [arXiv 2609.25913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25913)

**A5. Detecting that a procedure no longer works**
- **Memp** (procedural memory) [arXiv Aug 2025; listed as ACL 2026 by a third-party notes site, not verified]. Compares four update strategies: add everything, keep only validated successes, reflection-based in-place adjustment, and dynamic discarding. All help on TravelPlanner and ALFWorld, and reflection-based adjustment helps most. — [arXiv 2508.06433](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06433v3)
- **ToolEVO / ToolQA-D** [peer-reviewed, ICLR 2025; earlier edge of scope]. APIs change names, parameters and response formats. Statically trained tool use breaks, and MCTS exploration with self-reflection lets the model update its tool usage from environment feedback. One secondary summary reports +28.8% over static SFT on ToolQA-D-Hard OOD; I did not verify it. — [arXiv 2410.06617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06617v2)
- **TEPA, real file-backed execution drift**: revoking on contradicting execution evidence scores 0.950, against 0.203 for append-only. — [arXiv 2608.07429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07429)
- **Anthropic, Pokémon Red** [industry post]. A wrong note placed a PokéCenter on the wrong side of Mt. Moon. Claude fell off a ledge 3 times, then wrote that it would stop rather than keep looping, and never reached Cerulean City. After dreaming checked the note against transcripts and rewrote it, Claude reached Cerulean at action 1,935. The post says the transcript gave Claude ground truth to validate memory against. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)
- **Honest Lying (memory confabulation)** [workshop, ICML 2026 "Failure Modes in Agentic AI"]. Reflexion-style agents keep acting on confident but wrong self-diagnoses across trials even though the environment resets. In 16 ALFWorld environments where the agent never succeeded, none of 121 reflections named the correct target object, with 4 similar cases in HumanEval. Replacing open-ended self-diagnosis with programmatic extraction of trajectory-level failure signals raises correct-object mentions from 0% to 86% and lowers the Reflection Repetition Rate from 0.64 to 0.10, but solves only 3 of 16. — [arXiv 2605.29463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29463)

### Inferences
- **Scope and delivery.** Mainstream systems (Mem0, Zep, Memory-R1, A-Mem, MemoryOS, LightMem, Mem-α, HippoRAG 2) store no structured applicability scope; relevance is semantic similarity plus recency or heat. The closest analogues to "deliver only when scope covers the state" are AutoGuide's conditional guidelines with a context-identification step [earlier], TEPA's keyed precedents, and Veracium's per-fact validity intervals and source channels. CUPID (under 50% precision on which prior context applies) and Xiong et al. (similar-looking records that mislead) are direct evidence that similarity retrieval delivers out-of-scope memories. That supports making scope an explicit, code-checked field rather than leaving it to the retriever.
- **Narrow or demote on counter-evidence inside scope.** Close precedents: ExpeL's DOWNVOTE-to-zero [earlier], TEPA's revocation by same-key contradiction (kept for audit, re-promotable), Zep's `t_invalid` with history retained, and Hindsight's confidence updates on opinions. None of these narrows the scope (for example, "true for toolchain A, not B"). They invalidate or down-weight the whole record. Narrowing scope looks novel relative to what I found. STALE's finding that a change in one aspect of state should invalidate *related* memories, and StateMem's dependency modelling, suggest that counter-evidence should also travel along dependency edges, not stop at the record's own key.
- **Write-time vs use-time.** Several 2026 results suggest that correct supersession records do not ensure correct behaviour. Examples: STALE's recognition-vs-use gap, the stale-constraints study where provenance was reachable but inspected in about 1 of 5 episodes, and the Memory Trust Gap result that larger models are more fooled by a current-looking stale note. A delivery gate that withholds out-of-scope or revoked records (what CUPMem and TEPA do) targets this failure more directly than asking the model to judge validity. StateAuditor's design (an LLM proposes, deterministic code checks quote and chronology) is the closest published analogue to "quoted verbatim and checked by code", and its matched control credits the verification machinery rather than extra context.
- **Retirement after non-use.** Evidence for usage-based retirement is indirect: Xiong et al.'s retrieval-frequency deletion (part of a combined ~+10%), MemoryOS heat, FadeMem and MemoryBank decay. No paper I found isolated "retire after N sessions unused" as a variable. The Echo Gap and popularity-loop arguments warn that *use* is a poor proxy for *validity* when the memory itself shapes the sessions that use it. That bears directly on the design's own observation that model-lesson support came mostly from sessions that had received them.
- **Benchmark caution.** The MemoryAgentBench critique (last-write-wins solves 80%) and TEPA (only matches last-write-wins on SH-6k) imply that single-hop update benchmarks mostly reward a key-replace rule. Implicit-conflict (STALE), dependent-state (StateMemBench) and tenure (Veracium) benchmarks are better tests of a scoped design.

### Gaps
- I found no study that attaches a multi-field scope (project, toolchain, model, task class, user context) to each memory and measures delivery precision or behaviour change against similarity retrieval. AutoGuide's ablation is the nearest, and it is single-field and from 2024.
- I found no controlled study of a "retire after N unused sessions" rule. Decay papers (FadeMem, Oblivion, budgeted forgetting) report only snippet-level results, which I could not verify in full.
- Per-model numbers for STALE's recognition-vs-use gap and CUPMem's results were not in the abstract, and I did not read the full paper.
- I could not retrieve the title or confirm the venue type of the MemoryAgentBench critique.
- Mem0, MemoryBank, ExpeL and LongMemEval details are from prior knowledge and were not re-fetched. MemoryOS's "heat" formula and EMNLP 2025 venue were not re-verified (the arXiv page lists neither).
- "When Memories Become Liabilities" (arXiv 2603.04664), on prior answers biasing later steps, was cited only by a blog. I did not verify it.

## (B) Do lessons the model writes for itself help, compared with rules or preferences the user states?

### Takeaway
The two head-to-head comparisons I found both favour human-authored guidance. In SkillsBench (NeurIPS 2026), curated Skills add about +16pp while self-generated Skills average −1.3pp. In the AGENTS.md study, developer-written context files give small gains and LLM-generated ones slightly reduce success, while both add over 20% cost. Self-written memory can help a lot, but in the studies I found it helped when an external correctness signal (grading, environment success, an evaluator) gated what got written: Anthropic CL-Bench, self-generated in-context examples, AWM, ReasoningBank. It fails, inflates or confabulates when the signal is the model's own self-assessment (Echo Gap, Reflexion confabulation, intrinsic self-correction). Rigid rules about how to write memory also cut gains for strong models (Anthropic's "record only failures"). Inferring user preferences remains unreliable: about 50% or lower accuracy on PersonaMem and CUPID, and 37–48% for implicit preferences on PersonaMem-v2.

### Cited Findings

**B1. Direct comparisons: human-authored vs model-authored guidance**
- **SkillsBench** [peer-reviewed, NeurIPS 2026 poster; arXiv Feb 2026]. 84 tasks (86 in v1), each run with no Skills, curated Skills, or Skills the model wrote itself. Curated Skills raise the average pass rate by +16.2pp (v1) or +16.4pp (later version). Effects range from +4.5pp (software engineering) to +51.9pp (healthcare) in v1, and 16–18 of 84 tasks regress. **Self-generated Skills average −1.3pp.** The authors conclude current models "cannot author the procedural knowledge they benefit from consuming". The failure analysis finds self-written Skills coarse, incomplete, or missing the procedural details that verifiers check. One third-party breakdown gives 40.6% curated, 24.3% none and 21.0% self-generated, with self-generation run only on Claude Code and Codex (not verified). — [arXiv 2602.12670](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12670v1); [NeurIPS 2026 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/139353)
- **Evaluating AGENTS.md** [arXiv preprint v3 Sep 2026; ETH Zurich / Vechev group; pages also appear on the ICLR 2026 virtual site, likely a workshop, venue type unconfirmed]. Context files generally do not raise success on SWE-bench tasks or a new benchmark built from repos with developer-committed files, and they add over 20% inference cost. LLM-generated files tend to *lower* success compared with no file. They help only in an ablation where existing docs were removed, which suggests they mostly repeat documentation. Developer-written files beat generated ones but add only about +4% (secondary summary; exact per-condition numbers not verified). Agents follow the files' instructions closely, which leads to more exploration and testing. The authors recommend human files state only minimal requirements, and that such files are useful for "non-standard coding practices". — [arXiv 2602.11988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988); [summary](https://arxiviq.substack.com/p/evaluating-agentsmd-are-repository)
- **CIPHER / PRELUDE** [earlier; peer-reviewed, NeurIPS 2024]. Infers a natural-language preference from user edits, retrieves inferred preferences from the k closest past contexts, and re-infers when edit distance is large. An oracle given the true user preference is the upper bound. CIPHER has the lowest edit cost among learning methods (secondary summary: −31% summarization and −73% email vs no learning) but stays below the oracle. — [NeurIPS 2024 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/96078); [MSR page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/aligning-llm-agents-by-learning-latent-preference-from-user-edits/)

**B2. When self-written memory helps (an external signal gates it)**
- **Anthropic CL-Bench 1.0** [industry post; 6 Nov 2026]. A fresh session per question, with a persistent memory directory the model writes freely, against a stateless baseline. Mean reward without → with memory: Opus 5.5 0.655→0.838; Sonnet 5.5 0.644→0.833; Opus 5 0.564→0.828; Opus 4.7 0.383→0.700; Sonnet 5 0.306→0.610; Sonnet 4.6 0.330→0.364. Gains track whether the model "closes the learning loop": stronger models mark facts as checked or unconfirmed and record answers confirmed after grading, while Sonnet 4.6 kept scattered, untested guesses. The environment provides grading feedback. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)
- **Self-Generated In-Context Examples** [peer-reviewed, NeurIPS 2025]. Stores only the agent's own *successful* trajectories (filtered by environment success) as examples. ALFWorld 73%→89%, Wordcraft 55%→64%, InterCode-SQL 75%→79%. Adding curation (database-level population training plus per-example utility selection) reaches 93% on ALFWorld (91% in an earlier version). — [NeurIPS 2025](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/5d1f02132ef51602adf07000ca5b6138-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv 2505.00234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00234v3)
- **Agent Workflow Memory** [peer-reviewed, ICML 2025] (not re-fetched). Induces reusable workflows from the agent's own experience after filtering by an evaluator. +24.6% relative on Mind2Web and +51.1% on WebArena. — [arXiv 2409.07429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07429)
- **ReasoningBank** [peer-reviewed, ICLR 2026]. Distils strategy items from successes and pitfalls from failures, labelled by an LLM judge rather than ground truth. Adding failure trajectories helps, which is the opposite of a successes-only rule. The judge was 72.7% accurate on WebArena-Shopping, and simulated judges of 70–90% accuracy gave similar success. The authors note judge errors can contaminate memory, and retrieving too many experiences lowers success. — [arXiv 2509.25140](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.25140); [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/reasoningbank-enabling-agents-to-learn-from-experience/)
- **ACE (Agentic Context Engineering)** [peer-reviewed, ICLR 2026]. An evolving "playbook" with generation, reflection and curation steps and incremental delta updates. +10.6% on agent benchmarks and +8.6% on finance. It adapts without labels using execution feedback, and on AppWorld matches the top production agent with a smaller open model. — [arXiv 2510.04618](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04618)

**B3. When self-written memory hurts, saturates or misleads**
- **ACE's diagnosis of "context collapse" and "brevity bias"** [ICLR 2026] (numbers not re-fetched). When the model iteratively rewrites its whole context, detail erodes. In the paper's case study, a 18,282-token context at 66.7% accuracy collapsed in one step to 122 tokens at 57.1%, below the 63.7% no-adaptation baseline. — [arXiv 2510.04618](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04618)
- **Anthropic, steering memory writes** [industry post]. A fixed "record only failures" rule cut the memory gain by 0.234 (Opus 5), 0.150 (Opus 4.7) and 0.020 (Sonnet 4.6). The post concludes fixed rules can weaken stronger models: tell Claude what memory is for, not how to use it. The opposite holds for weaker models: telling Sonnet 4.6 to read memory and test its theories raised it from 0.364 to 0.641. Sonnet 5 read memory before answering 9/10 times unprompted, Sonnet 4.6 less than half the time. On salience, Sonnet 3.7 in Pokémon wrote 413 files in 225 folders (logging transient state the screen already shows) for 1 badge, while Claude Fable 5 wrote about 10 files for 4 badges. A dreaming-built wiki on one Harvey LAB task grew to 1.77× the corpus (203 KB vs 115 KB) and "effectively abstracted nothing". Four agents writing one shared table kept 41/57 contract facts (72%), while one page per contract quoting terms verbatim kept 57/57. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)
- **Experience-following and error propagation** (Xiong et al.) [peer-reviewed, ACL 2026]. High similarity between the current task and a retrieved record's task leads to high similarity in output. Errors in retrieved records are copied and amplified, and if the run is stored back, they spread to later tasks. — [arXiv 2505.16067](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067v2)
- **Memory Reward Inflation / Echo Gap** [arXiv-only; Jun 2026]. When episode scores are the agent's own self-assessment, wrong episodes get inflated scores and the agent preferentially reuses its *most confident mistakes*. The error compounds through memory, and a judge's errors stay correlated with the original self-grading bias, so an LLM judge cannot find the overvalued memories. This happens even under plain similarity retrieval. A usable signal must track truth and be decorrelated from the memory's bias (their "Error-Independence Assumption"). On BIRD text-to-SQL: no memory 52.4%, Memento-style self-graded memory 54.0%, and the LUCID de-inflation fix 56.9%. — [arXiv 2608.00017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00017)
- **Honest Lying** [workshop, ICML 2026]: self-generated Reflexion memories reinforce false beliefs. Details in A5. — [arXiv 2605.29463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29463)
- **MemoryBench** [arXiv-only; Oct 2025; Tsinghua]. Tests whether systems learn from (simulated) user feedback at service time and build procedural memory. Feedback improves performance on most datasets, but A-Mem, Mem0 and MemoryOS often fail to beat a simple RAG baseline that treats all feedback logs as documents. Learning curves fluctuate, especially in academic and legal domains. — [arXiv 2510.17281](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17281v1)
- **Reflexion** [earlier; peer-reviewed, NeurIPS 2023] (not re-fetched). Verbal self-reflections are stored in an episodic buffer. Gains depend on the evaluator signal, and it did not improve WebShop, which needs diverse exploration. — [arXiv 2303.11366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366)
- **"LLMs Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet"** [earlier; peer-reviewed, ICLR 2024] (not re-fetched). Without external feedback, intrinsic self-correction does not improve and can degrade reasoning. Earlier gains came partly from oracle stopping signals. — [arXiv 2310.01798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798). **"When Hindsight is Not 20/20"** [earlier; 2024]: feedback-free self-reflection helps TruthfulQA but hurts HotpotQA, and hurts when the first answer was right or the question easy. — [arXiv 2404.09129](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.09129)

**B4. Stated vs inferred user preferences**
- **PrefEval** [peer-reviewed, ICLR 2025 oral; Feb 2025]. 3,000 curated preference-query pairs across 20 topics, with preferences stated explicitly or implied (choice-based or persona-driven), in multi-session conversations up to 100k tokens. Zero-shot preference following falls **below 10% at just 10 turns (~3k tokens)** for most of the 10 models. Prompting and retrieval help but performance still degrades with length, and fine-tuning on PrefEval improves it markedly. Even explicitly stated preferences are not reliably followed without a dedicated mechanism. — [arXiv 2502.09597](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09597v1); [ICLR 2025 oral](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/oral/31848)
- **PersonaMem** [2025; listed on the NeurIPS 2025 virtual site; I recall COLM 2025 but did not re-verify the venue]. 180+ simulated histories of up to 60 sessions. Frontier models (GPT-4.1, o4-mini, GPT-4.5, o1, Gemini-2.0-Flash) reach about 50% accuracy (Llama-4-Maverick 43%). One summary gives 60–70% on recalling static facts and 30–50% on applying the user's latest state (25% is chance). — [arXiv 2504.14225](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14225v1); [NeurIPS 2025 page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/122431)
- **PersonaMem-v2** [arXiv-only; Dec 2025]. Frontier LLMs reach 37–48% on *implicit* personalization. An RL-fine-tuned Qwen3-4B reaches 53%, and the authors' agentic memory reaches 55% with 16× fewer input tokens. — [arXiv 2512.06688](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06688v1)
- **CUPID**: under 50% precision and under 65% recall when inferring contextual preferences (see A4). — [CUPID](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2508.01674)
- **ChatGPT memory audit, "The Algorithmic Self-Portrait"** [peer-reviewed, ACM Web Conference 2026]. 2,050 real memory entries from 80 users. **96% were created by the system without the user asking**, 28% contain GDPR-defined personal data, 52% contain psychological insights, and **84% are directly grounded in the user's conversation context**. The remaining roughly 16% are inferred or less directly supported; the abstract does not break this down. — [arXiv 2602.01450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01450)

### Inferences
- **Fit with the design's result.** The design found that user-stated rules produced nearly all measured behaviour gains while model-extracted lessons were mostly routine restatements. This matches the two cleanest external head-to-heads: SkillsBench (+16pp curated vs −1.3pp self-generated) and AGENTS.md (developer-written small gains, LLM-generated slight losses). SkillsBench's failure analysis ("coarse, incomplete, miss procedural idiosyncrasies") and AGENTS.md's ablation (generated files help only when docs are removed) describe the same "routine restatement" pattern: models write what they already know.
- **The important exception is gated self-learning.** Anthropic CL-Bench (+0.26 to +0.32 reward for strong models), self-generated in-context examples (+16pp ALFWorld) and AWM all show large gains from model-written memory. In each, an external truth signal (grading, environment success, an evaluator) filters or confirms what is written, and the stored item is often a task-specific fact or a verified trajectory rather than a general "lesson". This suggests the design's weak result may reflect lessons mined without a per-lesson correctness signal more than self-authorship as such. A fair comparison would separate "model-written and externally verified" from "model-written and self-assessed".
- **Circular support.** The design observed that model-lesson support came mostly from sessions that had received the lesson. That is the mechanism formalized as the Echo Gap (self-graded scores correlate with the memory's own bias) and seen empirically as experience-following and error propagation (Xiong et al.) and confabulation persistence (Honest Lying). Evidence for a lesson should come only from sessions that did *not* receive it, or from a counterfactual comparison. This matches the "Error-Independence Assumption" and the ExpeL/TEPA practice of counting contradictions against a record.
- **Rules about the memory process vs rules from the user.** Anthropic's "record only failures" result is evidence against *fixed write policies* imposed on strong models, not against user-stated task rules. The design's user rules are task and behaviour constraints, closer to SkillsBench's curated Skills and developer AGENTS.md. Anthropic's other findings fit the design's split: weaker models gain from explicit steering (Sonnet 4.6 0.364→0.641), and verbatim quoting beat paraphrase in shared pages (57/57 vs 41/57). The AGENTS.md authors' advice to keep human files to minimal requirements is a warning against letting the user-rule tier grow unchecked.
- **Inferred preferences.** Inferring preferences is wrong roughly half the time on current benchmarks (PersonaMem ~50%, PersonaMem-v2 37–48% implicit, CUPID under 50% precision). Even *stated* preferences are dropped without a dedicated mechanism (PrefEval below 10% at 10 turns zero-shot). This supports keeping user-stated rules verbatim and injecting them deterministically rather than relying on retrieval and in-context attention.

### Gaps
- I found no study measuring how often model-written *lessons* (as opposed to facts or trajectories) are restatements of what the model already knows. SkillsBench's qualitative failure analysis is the closest.
- I could not confirm exact per-condition success rates for the AGENTS.md study (LLM-generated vs developer-written vs none) or its peer-review status.
- I could not confirm from the abstract whether ACE reports degradation when ground-truth labels are absent, and the ACE collapse numbers were not re-fetched.
- I found no 2025–2026 controlled study isolating intrinsic (feedback-free) reflection in *agent memory*. Huang et al. and Li et al. are 2023–2024, and Honest Lying is a workshop paper.
- No source quantified how often ChatGPT-style inferred memories are factually *wrong* or outdated; the WWW 2026 audit measured grounding, not correctness.

## (C) Work that separates memory by provenance (user-stated vs inferred) and measures the difference

### Takeaway
Tagging memories by provenance is a common design proposal, and several 2026 systems keep provenance or epistemic type (Hindsight's fact/opinion networks, MemOS MemCube metadata, Veracium's source channels, TEPA's evidence-bound precedents). I found **no benchmark that compares downstream task utility of user-stated vs model-inferred memories**. The nearest measurements are indirect. The ChatGPT audit measures grounding (84% directly grounded, 96% system-created). Veracium finds a provenance-typed graph ages best and that injection resistance depends on provenance boundaries surviving representation. The Memory Trust Gap study shows removing the "memory" label increases over-trust while source authority barely matters.

### Cited Findings
- **ChatGPT memory audit** [WWW 2026]. Scores each memory on a five-point grounding scale, from directly stated through logically inferred to unsupported or contradicted, using an LLM judge. Aggregate: 84% directly grounded, 96% system-initiated. Provenance is measured, but not its effect on downstream behaviour. — [arXiv 2602.01450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01450)
- **Veracium / Ground Truth First** [arXiv-only; Jul 2026]. Synthetic life scripts tag each fact with source channel and sent-vs-received status (what the user said vs what others sent). Injection resistance depended on whether provenance boundaries survived the memory's representation. A provenance-typed graph reached 90% at 9 weeks while a curated map fell from 96% to 72%. A layered architecture led overall (96.8% at the short horizon). — [arXiv 2607.21962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21962)
- **Memory Trust Gap** [NeurIPS 2026 workshop submission]. Removing the provenance label ("memory") increases over-trust in stale notes at every scale, while source authority has small, scale-invariant effects. At 0.6B, 1.7B and 4B, models trust a stale *document* more than a stale *memory*, with no significant difference at 8B. Exposing metadata helps capable models only. — [arXiv 2609.01852](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01852)
- **Hindsight** [ACL 2026 demo]. Separates objective world facts and agent experiences from subjective opinions (with confidence) and observations. LongMemEval 83.6% (20B) to 91.4% (Gemini-3 Pro), self-reported. I found no ablation that isolates the value of the separation. — [arXiv 2512.12818](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2512.12818v1)
- **StateAuditor** [arXiv-only; Aug 2026]. Deterministic code binds each quoted value to one stored entry and checks that the new evidence is later. +5.0 points on STALE, while the matched control without the verification machinery gives only +0.6. This is the clearest measured evidence that code-checked quotes add value beyond extra context. — [arXiv 2608.01619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01619)
- **Inherited-memory stale constraints** [arXiv-only; Aug 2026]. Provenance links existed but agents followed them in about 1 of 5 episodes, so the mere presence of provenance did not change decisions. A rule that prioritized constraint-type memories did. — [arXiv 2608.25553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25553)
- **Design proposals (not research)**: a mem0 GitHub issue proposes a filterable `source_kind` (user-stated / observed / inferred) so inferred facts are never silently promoted to user-stated standing. A vta-agent-memory issue proposes that agent-written saves default to "inferred" unless the user stated them. — [mem0 issue #7782](https://github.com/mem0ai/mem0/issues/7782); [vta-agent-memory issue #32](https://github.com/OpenVTC/vta-agent-memory/issues/32)
- **Products**: Mem0's 2026 state-of-memory post notes filtering by participant and session to separate user-stated facts from agent inferences (vendor blog). — [Mem0 blog](https://mem0.ai/blog/state-of-ai-agent-memory-2026)

### Inferences
- The design's separation of verbatim, code-checked user rules from mined lessons lines up with an emerging consensus (Hindsight, MemOS, Veracium, TEPA, the mem0 proposal). But the design's own experiment, where gains concentrated in the user-stated tier, may be one of the few direct measurements of the utility gap between provenance classes. The closest external analogues are SkillsBench (curated vs self-generated) and AGENTS.md (developer vs LLM files), which compare authorship rather than memory provenance as such.
- The Memory Trust Gap and stale-constraints results suggest provenance should drive *gating* (what is delivered) rather than serve only as *metadata the model reads*. Models, especially larger ones, are swayed by recency cues and labels, and they rarely follow provenance links on their own.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed benchmark scoring task outcomes separately for user-stated vs inferred memories, or measuring the rate at which inferred memories get treated as user-stated.
- I found no evaluation of what Hindsight's fact/opinion separation contributes on its own.
- Commercial dual-tier memory products (explicit "saved memories" vs inferred chat-history memory) have no public quantitative comparison that I could find.
